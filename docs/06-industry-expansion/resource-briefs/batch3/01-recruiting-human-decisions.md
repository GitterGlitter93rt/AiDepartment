## Search Intent

What parts of recruiting can a business automate with AI, and what decisions should remain human?

## Core Thesis

The strongest recruiting use cases often involve repetitive coordination and information management rather than replacing human hiring judgment.

AI can help gather, organize, summarize, communicate, and schedule.

People should remain responsible for consequential employment decisions.

## Recommended Structure

### The Recruiting Workflow

Application
→ intake
→ initial screening
→ communication
→ recruiter review
→ interview
→ hiring decision
→ onboarding

### Good Candidates for AI Assistance

AI can support:

- applicant acknowledgment
- approved job-related screening questions
- candidate communication
- interview scheduling
- reminders
- transcript creation
- candidate-summary preparation
- recruiter task creation
- onboarding checklists
- internal recruiting reporting

### Where Humans Should Remain Responsible

Humans should remain responsible for:

- hiring decisions
- rejection decisions
- compensation decisions
- sensitive employment decisions
- accommodations
- unusual candidate situations
- interpreting subjective or high-context information

### Structured Screening

Explain why screening questions should focus on legitimate job-related information.

Avoid casually collecting sensitive or protected information.

### Human Review

AI-generated summaries can assist recruiters but should not be treated as infallible assessments.

### Implementation

Existing ATS
→ recruiting workflow
→ AI assistance
→ human review

If no ATS exists, evaluate whether the company needs:

- an ATS
- a lightweight recruiting database
- scheduling automation
- communication automation
- custom integration
