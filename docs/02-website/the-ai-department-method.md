# HERO

## A Practical System for Turning AI Into Business Results

Most businesses do not need more AI tools.

They need a process for determining where AI can actually create value, what should be implemented first, how it should connect to the business, and whether the investment is producing results.

The AI Department Method is our framework for moving from opportunity to implementation.

# THE PROBLEM

AI is moving quickly.

Every week brings:

- New AI models
- New software
- New agents
- New automation platforms
- New promises
- New vendors

That creates a problem for business leaders.

Which opportunities actually matter?

Which tools should you use?

What should you automate?

What should remain human?

What should you build?

What should you buy?

Where will AI actually produce ROI?

The AI Department Method provides a structured way to answer those questions.

---

# THE METHOD

The framework consists of six stages:

1. Assess
2. Discover
3. Prioritize
4. Implement
5. Adopt
6. Optimize

---

# STAGE 1 - ASSESS

## Understand Where You Are Today

The process begins by understanding the current business.

We evaluate areas such as:

- Leadership and AI strategy
- Marketing
- Lead generation
- Sales
- Customer service
- Operations
- Employee productivity
- Finance and administration
- Technology
- Data
- Existing AI adoption

The goal is not to find AI for the sake of AI.

The goal is to identify business problems and opportunities.

# STAGE 2 - DISCOVER

## Understand How the Business Actually Works

High-value AI implementation requires more than an online questionnaire.

For deeper engagements, discovery may examine:

- Department workflows
- Employee responsibilities
- Software
- Data
- Customer journeys
- Lead flow
- Sales processes
- Reporting
- Administrative work
- Financial impact
- Business goals

We want to understand where:

- Time is being lost
- Revenue is being lost
- Customers are waiting
- Employees are overloaded
- Information is trapped
- Work is unnecessarily repetitive
- Systems are disconnected
- Growth requires additional overhead

# WORKFLOW DISCOVERY

A workflow may be mapped as:

Trigger

↓

Information received

↓

Employee action

↓

Software action

↓

Decision

↓

Follow-up

↓

Outcome

Then we ask:

Which steps require human judgment?

Which steps are repetitive?

Which steps can be automated?

Which steps can AI assist?

Which steps should remain entirely human?

---

# STAGE 3 - PRIORITIZE

## Find the Opportunities Worth Pursuing

Not every automation deserves to be built.

Not every AI tool deserves to be purchased.

Opportunities should be prioritized based on factors such as:

- Financial impact
- Employee time
- Revenue potential
- Customer impact
- Implementation cost
- Implementation difficulty
- Data availability
- Risk
- Speed to value
- Strategic importance

---

# OPPORTUNITY MATRIX

Potential opportunities can be classified as:

## Quick Wins

High value

Low complexity

Implement first.

---

## Strategic Projects

High value

Higher complexity

Plan carefully.

---

## Efficiency Improvements

Moderate value

Low complexity

Implement when resources permit.

---

## Low-Priority Experiments

Low value or uncertain ROI

Do not allow these to distract from better opportunities.

---

# ROI BEFORE HYPE

Whenever sufficient data exists, we attempt to quantify the business case.

Potential measurements include:

- Employee hours recovered
- Labor capacity
- Overtime reduced
- Future hiring avoided
- Leads recovered
- Sales conversion improvement
- Advertising efficiency
- Customer response time
- Reporting time
- Working-capital improvement

The objective is not to create the largest ROI number.

The objective is to create a defensible business case.

---

# STAGE 4 - IMPLEMENT

## Turn the Strategy Into Working Systems

Once an opportunity is validated, implementation begins.

Depending on the project, this may involve:

- AI agents
- Workflow automation
- CRM automation
- Software integration
- Internal AI assistants
- AI phone agents
- Marketing systems
- Reporting dashboards
- Custom software
- Existing SaaS platforms
- API integrations

The best solution may involve AI.

It may also involve ordinary automation or better software.

We use the technology that best solves the business problem.

---

# BUILD VS BUY

Before custom development, we evaluate:

Can existing software solve the problem?

Can the current software be configured differently?

Can systems be connected through APIs?

Can an automation platform solve it?

Does the business actually require custom development?

Custom software should be built when the business case justifies it, not because custom development sounds impressive.

---

# HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP

Some workflows should retain human review.

Examples may include:

- Legal decisions
- Financial approvals
- Medical decisions
- Hiring and termination
- Regulatory decisions
- Safety-critical operations
- Significant financial commitments
- Sensitive customer decisions

Good AI implementation defines where machines assist and where humans remain responsible.

---

# STAGE 5 - ADOPT

## Technology Only Creates Value When People Use It

Implementation is not finished when software goes live.

Employees may need:

- Training
- Documentation
- Prompt libraries
- SOPs
- AI usage policies
- Role-specific examples
- Support
- Leadership guidance

Adoption should be treated as part of implementation.

---

# EMPLOYEE TRAINING

Training may cover:

- Approved AI tools
- Prompting
- Role-specific use cases
- Verification
- Privacy
- Security
- Workflow changes
- Human review

The goal is practical adoption.

---

# LEADERSHIP ADOPTION

Leadership must understand:

- Why the system exists
- What it should improve
- What employees are expected to do
- What metrics matter
- What risks exist
- Who owns the process

Without ownership, AI initiatives can become abandoned experiments.

---

# STAGE 6 - OPTIMIZE

## Measure What Happened

After implementation, we evaluate results.

Potential measurements include:

- Hours saved
- Response time
- Lead conversion
- Cost per acquisition
- Customer satisfaction
- Employee adoption
- Error rates
- Processing time
- Revenue
- Cost reduction
- Capacity created

# THE MANAGED AI DEPARTMENT

Companies with ongoing AI opportunities may not want to build an internal AI leadership team immediately.

The Managed AI Department can provide ongoing support around:

- AI strategy
- Opportunity discovery
- Implementation management
- Vendor evaluation
- Employee training
- AI governance
- Automation
- Performance measurement
- Technology evaluation
- Roadmap updates

This creates an ongoing AI capability without necessarily requiring a full internal department.

# WHERE MARKETING FITS

AI transformation is not limited to cost reduction.

Revenue growth matters too.

The AI Department Method can evaluate:

- Google Ads
- Meta Ads
- SEO
- Landing pages
- CRM
- Lead response
- Sales follow-up
- AI phone agents
- Customer reactivation
- Conversion tracking
- Revenue attribution

A company may discover that its highest-value AI opportunity is improving how it acquires and converts customers.

---

# WHERE EMPLOYEE PRODUCTIVITY FITS

AI may help employees:

- Research faster
- Write faster
- Find information faster
- Create reports faster
- Respond to customers faster
- Document processes
- Prepare proposals
- Analyze information
- Reduce repetitive administration

Sometimes the best ROI comes from helping existing employees accomplish more.

---

# WHERE AI AGENTS FIT

AI agents may be appropriate when a workflow has:

- Repetitive inputs
- Clear objectives
- Accessible information
- Predictable actions
- Sufficient volume
- Measurable business value

Potential agents include:

- AI receptionist
- Sales assistant
- Lead qualification agent
- Customer service agent
- Employee knowledge agent
- Reporting agent
- Scheduling agent
- Document-processing agent
- Marketing agent
- Finance support agent

Agents should be evaluated individually rather than deployed simply because they are fashionable.

---

# WHAT MAKES THE METHOD DIFFERENT

The AI Department Method begins with the business.

Not the tool.

We ask:

What is the problem?

What is it costing?

What would improvement be worth?

What is the simplest practical solution?

How should it be implemented?

How will employees use it?

How will we measure whether it worked?

That keeps AI connected to business outcomes.
