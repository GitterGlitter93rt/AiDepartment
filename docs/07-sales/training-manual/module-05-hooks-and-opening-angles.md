# Module 5 - Hooks and Opening Angles

Status: Working Draft
Purpose: Internal sales training

---

# MODULE PURPOSE

A hook is not a pitch.

A hook is a short, relevant business question or observation that earns enough attention to begin discovery.

The salesperson is not trying to make the prospect believe in AI during the opening seconds.

The salesperson is trying to make the prospect think:

> That is actually a good question.

The best hooks point toward a business pressure point such as:

- lost opportunities
- slow response
- weak follow-up
- employee capacity
- marketing efficiency
- customer experience
- reporting visibility
- disconnected systems
- scalability
- cost of delay

The hook should make the prospect want to explain how the business works.

---

# 1. THE YAD HOOK FORMULA

Use this mental structure:

## CONTEXT

Show that the question is relevant to the type of business.

## PRESSURE POINT

Point toward a process where value may leak.

## QUESTION

Ask something the prospect can answer without needing a presentation.

Example:

> You guys advertise pretty heavily around emergency HVAC. When a call comes in after hours and nobody on the office team can grab it, what happens next?

Context:

Emergency HVAC.

Pressure point:

After-hours lead handling.

Question:

What happens next?

That is a hook.

---

# 2. THE SEVEN RULES OF A STRONG HOOK

A strong YAD hook should usually be:

1. Specific enough to feel relevant.
2. Short enough to understand immediately.
3. Focused on one business problem.
4. Easy to answer.
5. Non-accusatory.
6. Free of unsupported statistics or guarantees.
7. About the business before it is about AI.

Weak:

> Did you know AI can save businesses millions of dollars?

Strong:

> When a new lead does not answer the first callback, does your team have a defined follow-up process or does it depend on the salesperson?

---

# 3. NEVER TURN THE HOOK INTO AN ACCUSATION

Avoid:

> You are probably losing a ton of money from missed calls.

Better:

> Do you know roughly what happens to calls your office cannot answer immediately?

Avoid:

> Your employees are wasting time doing manual work.

Better:

> What repetitive office task takes more employee time than you think it should?

Avoid:

> Your marketing agency is wasting your budget.

Better:

> Can you currently trace your marketing spend from the original lead all the way to collected revenue?

Ask first.

Diagnose second.

Quantify third.

---

# 4. PHONE HOOKS VS IN-PERSON HOOKS

The underlying business problem may be identical.

The delivery should change.

## PHONE

The hook has to create relevance before the prospect decides to end the call.

Example:

> Quick question - what happens to an estimate that does not close on the first conversation?

## IN PERSON

The rep can use a respectful observation or business-specific context.

Example:

> I noticed the phones stay pretty active up front. Do you guys have a way to track calls the office cannot grab, or is that hard to see?

In person, never use an observation to insult the business.

Observation should create a question.

Not a conclusion.

---

# 5. HOOK FAMILY 1 - MONEY LEAKAGE

## When to use

Use when the business has high-value leads, estimates, appointments, cases, treatments, contracts, repair orders, or other measurable opportunities.

## Phone examples

> What happens to a quote that does not close the first time?

> Do you know how many estimates are still sitting unsold at the end of a normal month?

> How many legitimate leads do you think disappear somewhere between inquiry and booked customer?

> If I asked you where the biggest revenue leak is between lead and sale, where would you guess?

> Do you know the approximate value of the opportunities that never receive a second follow-up?

## In-person examples

> I am curious - once somebody leaves with an estimate, who owns the follow-up from there?

> When a customer says they need to think about it, is there a standard process after that or does each salesperson handle it differently?

> Do you guys have a clean way of seeing unsold opportunities by dollar value?

## Best follow-up

> About how many of those happen in a normal month?

Then:

> What is one legitimate customer normally worth?

Do not calculate until the inputs are credible.

---

# 6. HOOK FAMILY 2 - SPEED TO LEAD

## When to use

Use for businesses where customers often contact multiple providers or have urgent needs.

Common examples:

- HVAC
- plumbing
- electrical
- roofing
- restoration
- collision
- legal intake
- med spa inquiries
- dental emergencies
- home services

## Phone examples

> How quickly does a brand-new inbound lead normally hear from somebody?

> What happens when a lead comes in while everybody is already tied up?

> After hours, does a new inquiry get a response right away or wait until the next business day?

> Is response time something you actually measure or mostly something you assume is happening?

> When somebody submits a form online at night, what is the first thing that happens?

## In-person examples

> If I submitted your website form right now, what would happen next?

> When three calls hit at the same time, how do you make sure the third opportunity does not disappear?

## Best follow-up

> Do you know roughly how many leads per month come through that channel?

---

# 7. HOOK FAMILY 3 - MISSED CALLS AND AFTER-HOURS

## When to use

Use for phone-driven businesses and businesses that advertise heavily.

## Phone examples

> What happens to a legitimate new-business call that reaches voicemail?

> Do you know roughly what percentage of inbound calls nobody gets to during business hours?

> What happens after hours today - voicemail, answering service, on-call person, something else?

> If the front office is busy with a customer and another new prospect calls, how is that opportunity recovered?

> Do missed calls automatically create a text, task or callback workflow today?

## In-person examples

> Your phones look pretty active. Do you have visibility into the calls that never get answered?

> If this desk gets slammed for twenty minutes, is there any backup process for new calls?

## Best follow-up

> Of the calls you miss, what percentage would you guess are actually legitimate new customers?

Never assume every call equals lost revenue.

---

# 8. HOOK FAMILY 4 - FOLLOW-UP CONSISTENCY

## When to use

Use when selling requires multiple touches.

## Phone examples

> Who owns follow-up after the first attempt does not connect?

> How many follow-up attempts does a typical lead receive before the team stops?

> Is your follow-up process standardized or does it depend on the individual salesperson?

> What happens to a lead that says, "Call me next month"?

> Can you see which leads have gone untouched for seven, fourteen or thirty days?

## In-person examples

> If I asked one salesperson here and another salesperson here how they follow up, would I hear the same process?

> What happens to the leads nobody is actively working anymore?

## Best follow-up

> Roughly how many open or old opportunities are sitting in the system right now?

---

# 9. HOOK FAMILY 5 - MARKETING EFFICIENCY AND ATTRIBUTION

## When to use

Use when the company spends meaningfully on Google Ads, Meta, SEO, lead vendors, direct mail, or other acquisition channels.

## Phone examples

> Can you trace a marketing dollar all the way from the ad to the lead to the actual sale?

> Which marketing channel produces your most profitable customers right now?

> Do you know your cost per booked customer, or mainly your cost per lead?

> When a lead comes from Google or Facebook, can you see whether it eventually became revenue?

> If I asked which campaigns generated collected revenue last month, how quickly could you answer?

## In-person examples

> I saw you guys advertising pretty aggressively locally. Do you have clean revenue attribution behind it or is there still some guesswork?

> You are obviously generating demand. I am curious whether you can see what happens after the lead hits the office.

## Best follow-up

> What are you spending in a normal month?

Then:

> What do you trust most in your current tracking?

Do not call spend wasted before an audit proves it.

---

# 10. HOOK FAMILY 6 - EMPLOYEE CAPACITY

## When to use

Use when employees perform repetitive administrative, communication, reporting, scheduling, CRM, document, or data-movement work.

## Phone examples

> What repetitive task would your office team happily never do manually again?

> What work keeps good employees busy that does not really require their judgment?

> Where are employees copying the same information from one system into another?

> What administrative task eats the most hours every week?

> If you could give your best office employee five hours back every week, where would you want that time spent?

## In-person examples

> I am not looking to replace anybody here. I am curious what repetitive work keeps the team from spending more time with customers.

> Where does the office get buried when volume spikes?

## Best follow-up

> How many employees touch that process and about how much time does it take?

Correct framing:

Employee capacity.

Not automatic payroll savings.

---

# 11. HOOK FAMILY 7 - GROWTH AND SCALABILITY

## When to use

Use with companies that are growing, adding locations, adding sales volume, entering new markets, or experiencing seasonal surges.

## Phone examples

> If revenue grew 50% next year, where would the operation break first?

> Where would you have to add administrative headcount first if volume doubled?

> What process works fine at your current size but probably does not work at twice the volume?

> Is your growth currently limited more by demand or by the team's ability to handle the demand?

> Which department feels the pain first when business gets busy?

## In-person examples

> You guys look busy. If another twenty percent of volume showed up next month, where would the bottleneck hit first?

> What would you need to hire for first if this location got dramatically busier?

## Best follow-up

> What work would that new hire actually be doing?

This can reveal workflow opportunities without positioning YAD as replacing employees.

---

# 12. HOOK FAMILY 8 - REPORTING AND VISIBILITY

## When to use

Use when leadership lacks timely access to operational or financial information.

## Phone examples

> What is one business number you wish you could see instantly but still have to ask somebody to build?

> How long does it take leadership to get a clean weekly performance report?

> Do your managers spend time assembling reports or actually analyzing them?

> Can you see lead response, conversion and revenue by source in one place today?

> Which KPI is hardest to trust right now?

## In-person examples

> If the owner asked for yesterday's leads, booked jobs and revenue by source right now, could the team pull it quickly?

## Best follow-up

> How many people touch that reporting process and how often is it rebuilt?

---

# 13. HOOK FAMILY 9 - SYSTEM DISCONNECTION

## When to use

Use when the company appears to use multiple systems such as CRM, scheduling, accounting, phone, field-service, estimating, case management, marketing, or spreadsheets.

## Phone examples

> Which two systems in your company should talk to each other but still do not?

> Where does somebody manually move information from one system into another?

> Does your CRM automatically know what happened on the phone, or does somebody have to enter it?

> What data gets retyped most often?

> Where do things fall through the cracks because one system does not know what happened in another?

## In-person examples

> I am curious - when this form gets completed, does the information automatically reach the next system or does somebody re-enter it?

Only ask observation-based questions when you can do so without viewing confidential information.

---

# 14. HOOK FAMILY 10 - CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE

## When to use

Use for appointment-heavy, service-heavy, communication-heavy businesses.

## Phone examples

> What customer question does your team answer fifty times a week?

> What part of the customer journey creates the most inbound status calls?

> Where do customers usually get frustrated waiting for an update?

> What communication could be proactive instead of waiting for the customer to ask?

> What happens when a customer needs to reschedule outside office hours?

## In-person examples

> What question does your front desk get tired of answering over and over every day?

## Best follow-up

> How much staff time do you think that communication consumes?

---

# 15. HOOK FAMILY 11 - COMPETITIVE PRESSURE

Competitive hooks must be used carefully.

Do not invent what competitors are doing.

Bad:

> Your competitors are all using AI already.

Unless we can prove that statement, do not say it.

Better:

> In a market where customers may contact several providers, how important is being the first company to respond?

> What do you think your best competitor does better operationally than most companies in the market?

> If two companies generate the same number of leads, what determines which one converts more of them?

> Where do you think your customer experience is strongest versus competitors, and where could it be faster?

Competitive pressure should create reflection.

Not fabricated fear.

---

# 16. HOOK FAMILY 12 - COST OF DELAY

Use only after a problem is credible.

Do not begin with fake urgency.

Useful questions:

> How long has this been a known problem?

> What happens if the company keeps doing it this way for another twelve months?

> Is the problem becoming larger as volume grows?

> What would have to happen for fixing this to become a priority?

> If we could prove the financial impact is material, how quickly would you want to address it?

The cost-of-delay hook comes later in the conversation.

Not as a scare tactic in the first sentence.

---

# 17. THE HOOK LADDER

A hook should naturally progress into discovery.

Example:

## HOOK

> What happens to calls after hours?

## PROCESS

> Voicemail.

## FREQUENCY

> About how many calls hit voicemail in a normal week?

## QUALIFICATION

> How many of those would you guess are legitimate new-business opportunities?

## VALUE

> What is a typical new customer worth?

## CURRENT RESPONSE

> What happens the next morning?

## IMPACT

> Is recovering more of those calls something you would care about if the numbers were meaningful?

That is the bridge from hook to diagnosis.

---

# 18. IF THE HOOK MISSES

Not every hypothesis will be correct.

Rep:

> Do you guys struggle with missed calls after hours?

Owner:

> No. We have a live answering team twenty-four seven and they are excellent.

Wrong response:

> Well, AI could replace that.

Correct response:

> Good - then I would leave that alone. What part of the operation feels the most manual or inconsistent right now?

This proves we are diagnosing, not forcing a predetermined product.

---

# 19. SAFE PATTERN INTERRUPTS

A pattern interrupt is useful when it lowers resistance without becoming gimmicky.

Approved examples:

> This is a cold call, so I will be brief.

> I do not know yet whether this is relevant to you.

> I have one question and you can tell me if I am completely off base.

> I am not calling to sell you a chatbot.

> I am trying to understand one part of your workflow.

> I do not want to send you generic AI garbage, so let me ask one question first.

Avoid weird, manipulative, or overly scripted tricks.

We want competent adults talking to business owners.

---

# 20. RESEARCH-BASED HOOKS

Public research can make a hook feel specific.

Examples:

> I saw that you offer 24/7 emergency service. What actually happens to calls after the main office closes?

> I saw you have three locations. Is lead handling centralized or does each location manage its own inquiries?

> I noticed you are running a fairly large service area. How do you route new opportunities to the right team?

> I saw you offer financing. What happens to customers who receive a proposal but do not move forward immediately?

> I noticed you are hiring office staff. Is that mainly because volume is increasing or because the current workflows are too manual?

Never pretend public research proves an internal problem.

Use it to ask a better question.

---

# 21. OBSERVATION-BASED FIELD HOOKS

In-person sales creates information the phone does not.

Possible observations:

- busy phones
- waiting customers
- obvious paper-based intake
- many front-desk interruptions
- multiple visible software screens without reading confidential data
- sales/estimate boards intentionally visible to visitors
- high customer traffic
- signs promoting emergency service
- hiring signs
- multiple service vehicles

Examples:

> You guys stay busy up front. When all the lines are occupied, how do you recover new calls that cannot get answered?

> I noticed you have multiple locations listed. Does each location handle follow-up separately?

> You clearly have a lot of customer communication happening here. What question generates the most repetitive calls?

Do not photograph, inspect, read, or reference private customer information.

---

# 22. GATEKEEPER HOOKS

The goal is not to trick reception.

The goal is to help reception identify the appropriate person.

Examples:

> Who normally owns lead follow-up here?

> Who would be responsible for how calls, CRM and scheduling connect?

> Who handles the operational side of your sales process?

> Who would know how after-hours inquiries are handled?

> Who owns marketing attribution and lead reporting?

> Who is responsible for process improvement across the office?

This is often better than repeatedly asking:

> Is the owner available?

---

# 23. ULTRA-SHORT HOOKS FOR BUSY OWNERS

If the owner gives you ten seconds, use one question.

> Do you know what happens to missed calls here?

> Who follows up unsold estimates?

> Can you track ad spend to revenue?

> What office task wastes the most time?

> If volume doubled, where would you need to hire first?

> What happens to leads after hours?

> Do all new leads make it into the CRM?

> How many old leads are sitting untouched?

> What customer question does the team answer constantly?

> Which two systems should be connected but are not?

Do not cram three hooks into ten seconds.

# 25. HOW TO SELECT THE RIGHT HOOK

Before a call or walk-in, choose ONE primary hypothesis.

Use this hierarchy:

## 1. VISIBLE OR RESEARCHED RELEVANCE

Example:

24/7 service -> after-hours hook.

## 2. INDUSTRY-LOGICAL PRESSURE POINT

Example:

Roofing -> estimate follow-up.

## 3. COMPANY-SPECIFIC GROWTH SIGNAL

Example:

Multiple new locations -> scalability hook.

## 4. GENERAL DIAGNOSTIC HOOK

Example:

> What repetitive process would you fix first if you could?

Do not randomly pick the most dramatic hook.

Pick the most plausible one.

# 27. WHAT NOT TO USE AS A HOOK

Avoid:

> AI is going to change everything.

> Your competitors are replacing their staff with AI.

> You are losing $100,000 a month.

> We can cut your payroll.

> Your receptionist is costing you leads.

> Your marketing agency is wasting money.

> I can guarantee we will increase your revenue.

> Every missed call is lost revenue.

> We can automate your whole company.

> We have an AI solution for everything.

These statements are either generic, inflammatory, unsupported, or inconsistent with YAD doctrine.

# 28. BUILDING YOUR OWN HOOK

Reps should eventually create their own hooks using this template:

> I noticed / I was curious about [business context]. When [specific event happens], what happens next?

Examples:

> I noticed you have three service locations. When a lead comes in, how does it get routed to the right location?

> I saw you offer emergency service. When calls come in after hours, what happens next?

> I saw you are hiring another coordinator. What workload is driving that hire?

Alternative template:

> How do you currently handle [specific workflow] when [pressure condition]?

Example:

> How do you currently handle estimate follow-up when a salesperson is carrying fifty open opportunities?

---

# 29. THE THREE TESTS BEFORE USING A HOOK

Ask yourself:

## TEST 1 - COULD THIS BE TRUE?

Is there a reasonable basis for asking the question?

## TEST 2 - CAN THE PROSPECT ANSWER IT?

If the question requires five minutes of explanation before it makes sense, it is not a good opening hook.

## TEST 3 - DOES THE ANSWER LEAD SOMEWHERE?

The hook should connect to:

- a measurable business problem
- a relevant YAD capability
- or useful disqualification information

# 30. ROLEPLAY DRILL - RAPID HOOK SELECTION

Manager gives the rep a company profile.

Example:

> Three-location HVAC company. Runs Google Ads. Offers emergency service. Hiring two dispatchers.

Rep has ten seconds to select a hook.

Possible answer:

> You guys offer emergency service across three locations. When an after-hours lead comes in, how is it routed and how quickly does somebody respond?

Then manager changes the profile.

> Small law firm. Heavy Google Ads. Intake closes at 5 PM.

Rep:

> What happens to a prospective client who calls from one of those ads at 7 PM?

Continue until hook selection becomes instinctive.

---

# 31. ROLEPLAY DRILL - PHONE VS FIELD

The rep must take the same business problem and deliver it two ways.

Problem:

Unsold roofing estimates.

## PHONE

> Quick question - what happens to an estimate that does not sign during the first presentation?

## FIELD

> I do not want to interrupt the office. I had one question about your sales process: once a homeowner leaves without signing, who owns the follow-up from there?

The content is similar.

The social context is different.

---

# 32. HOOK PERFORMANCE SHOULD BE MEASURED

Hooks are not sacred scripts.

Track:

- hook used
- industry
- channel: phone or field
- decision-maker reached
- conversation opened
- meaningful pain identified
- meeting earned
- disqualification

Over time, YAD should know which hooks actually produce meaningful conversations by industry.

Do not optimize only for people who stay on the phone.

Optimize for qualified business conversations.

# 34. MODULE 5 CERTIFICATION

A salesperson passes when they can:

- explain the difference between a hook and a pitch
- choose a relevant hook based on company context
- deliver the hook naturally by phone
- adapt the same hook for an in-person walk-in
- avoid unsupported claims
- avoid attacking employees
- ask one useful follow-up question
- move from hook to process, frequency and value
- abandon a bad hypothesis when the prospect disproves it
- create a new compliant hook without manager assistance

Certification exercise:

The manager gives the salesperson ten random prospect profiles.

For each prospect, the rep must provide:

1. Primary hook.
2. Backup hook.
3. First discovery question.
4. Financial follow-up question.
5. Correct ROI worksheet if the issue is validated.
6. Employee-safe language if labor capacity is involved.

Passing standard:

The rep sounds like a business diagnostician.

Not a script reader.

# MODULE 5 CORE RULE

# THE HOOK DOES NOT SELL YOUR AI DEPARTMENT.

# THE HOOK EARNS THE RIGHT TO UNDERSTAND THE BUSINESS.

Once the owner starts explaining the workflow, the salesperson has moved from interruption into discovery.

That is the win.
