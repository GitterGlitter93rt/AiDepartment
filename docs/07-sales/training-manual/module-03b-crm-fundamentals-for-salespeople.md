# MODULE 3B - CRM FUNDAMENTALS FOR SALESPEOPLE

Status: Working Draft - Internal Sales Training

## Purpose

Every Your AI Department salesperson must understand what a CRM is, why it matters, and how CRM systems connect to the broader YAD value proposition.

A rep does not need to be a CRM administrator.

A rep does need to be able to explain, in plain business language, why a company may need one and what can become possible when the CRM is designed correctly.

---

# 1. WHAT DOES CRM MEAN?

CRM stands for:

CUSTOMER RELATIONSHIP MANAGEMENT.

In practical terms, a CRM is a central system for managing leads, prospects, customers, sales opportunities, conversations, tasks, follow-up, pipeline stages, and business-development activity.

A good CRM helps answer questions such as:

- Who is this lead?
- Where did the lead come from?
- Who owns the lead?
- Has anyone called them?
- What did they say?
- What stage are they in?
- What needs to happen next?
- When should someone follow up?
- Did they book?
- Did they buy?
- How much revenue did they produce?
- Which marketing source generated them?
- Why was the opportunity won or lost?

Without a CRM, this information often lives across:

- employee memory
- email inboxes
- text messages
- sticky notes
- spreadsheets
- personal phones
- paper forms
- disconnected software

That makes follow-up, accountability, attribution, and reporting much harder.

---

# 2. THE SIMPLEST WAY TO EXPLAIN A CRM

If a prospect asks what a CRM is, the rep can say:

> A CRM is basically the central system that keeps every lead, customer, conversation, follow-up task, and sales stage from living in somebody's head, inbox, phone, or spreadsheet.

Short version:

> It is the system that keeps track of who the customer is, where they came from, what happened, and what needs to happen next.

Do not make the explanation more technical than necessary.

# 5. WHAT A WELL-DESIGNED CRM CAN DO

A CRM can be much more than a digital address book.

Potential capabilities include:

## LEAD CAPTURE

Automatically create lead records from:

- website forms
- landing pages
- Meta lead forms
- Google Ads leads
- inbound calls
- chat
- referrals
- manual entry
- imported databases

## LEAD ROUTING

Assign leads based on:

- location
- service
- salesperson
- territory
- availability
- department
- lead type
- round robin rules

## PIPELINE MANAGEMENT

Create visible stages such as:

New Lead
->
Contacted
->
Qualified
->
Appointment Set
->
Estimate / Proposal Sent
->
Follow-Up
->
Won
->
Lost

Stages must reflect the actual business.

## AUTOMATED FOLLOW-UP

Trigger appropriate:

- SMS
- email
- reminders
- call tasks
- salesperson notifications
- appointment reminders
- estimate follow-up
- no-response follow-up
- long-term nurture

## MISSED-CALL WORKFLOWS

Depending on the system and phone configuration:

Missed call
->
lead record / activity
->
automatic SMS
->
callback task
->
assignment
->
follow-up tracking

## APPOINTMENT MANAGEMENT

Potentially support:

- booking
- confirmation
- reminders
- rescheduling
- no-show follow-up
- salesperson / technician notifications

## ESTIMATE / PROPOSAL FOLLOW-UP

Track:

- estimate sent
- date sent
- value
- next follow-up
- customer response
- salesperson owner
- won / lost status

## REACTIVATION

Segment old:

- leads
- estimates
- customers
- inactive accounts
- overdue maintenance customers

and create compliant reactivation workflows where appropriate.

## ATTRIBUTION

Track where practical:

Ad
->
Lead
->
Qualified Lead
->
Appointment
->
Sale
->
Revenue

This allows management to ask a better question than:

> How many leads did Facebook generate?

The better question is:

> How much customer revenue did those leads ultimately produce?

## DASHBOARDS AND REPORTING

Potential reporting may include:

- leads by source
- leads by salesperson
- response time
- conversion rate
- pipeline value
- estimate close rate
- appointments booked
- no-show rate
- sales by source
- revenue by source
- lost reasons
- follow-up compliance
- salesperson activity
- location performance

## AI-ASSISTED CRM WORK

Where appropriate, AI may help with:

- call summaries
- meeting summaries
- note generation
- follow-up drafting
- lead classification
- data extraction
- pipeline summaries
- management summaries

# 6. A CRM DOES NOT FIX A BAD PROCESS BY ITSELF

A company can own an expensive CRM and still have terrible follow-up.

The rep should understand:

SOFTWARE
+
PROCESS
+
ADOPTION
+
AUTOMATION
+
MEASUREMENT
=
USEFUL CRM SYSTEM

Buying software is not the same as implementing a sales operating system.

This is why the question:

> Do you have a CRM?

is only the beginning.

Follow with:

> How consistently is it actually used?

> What happens automatically after a new lead enters it?

> Can you see which opportunities have not been touched recently?

> Can you track a lead all the way to revenue?

Then diagnose:

- How are leads tracked today?
- Who owns follow-up?
- How are tasks remembered?
- How does management see pipeline?
- How are lead sources tracked?
- Where are customer conversations stored?
- How are old leads reactivated?

YAD can help businesses evaluate, select, configure, and implement CRM systems where appropriate.

Do not promise that one specific platform is correct before discovery.

Do not promise every integration before technical review.

# 9. CRM SALES HOOKS

## NO CRM

> How are new leads tracked today after they come in?

> If I asked you for every open opportunity right now, where would you look?

> How does management know a salesperson followed up?

## POOR CRM ADOPTION

> Does the CRM show reality, or do people still keep important information outside it?

> If a salesperson leaves tomorrow, how much customer history leaves with them?

## WEAK FOLLOW-UP

> Can your CRM automatically show every opportunity that has not been touched in seven days?

> What happens after the first follow-up attempt fails?

## ATTRIBUTION

> Can your CRM tell you which advertising source produced the customer and revenue?

## CAPACITY

> How much salesperson or office time is spent manually entering CRM notes and updates?

# 15. THE CRM DIAGNOSTIC QUESTIONS

Every rep should know these:

1. Do you currently use a CRM?
2. Which system?
3. Does the team actually use it consistently?
4. How do new leads enter it?
5. Are leads automatically assigned?
6. What happens after the first contact attempt?
7. Can management see untouched leads?
8. Can you track lead source?
9. Can you track source through to sale / revenue?
10. Are calls tracked?
11. Are texts and emails visible in the customer record?
12. Are follow-up tasks automated or manual?
13. Can you see pipeline value?
14. Can you see why deals are lost?
15. How are old leads reactivated?
16. What reporting is still manually assembled?
17. What CRM work do employees hate doing?
18. Which systems should connect to the CRM but do not?

Do not ask all eighteen at once.

Use the relevant questions during discovery.

---

# 16. WHAT THE REP SHOULD NOT PROMISE

Do not promise:

- every platform integrates perfectly
- migration will be simple before inspection
- all historical data can be cleaned automatically
- every communication can be automated
- the CRM will automatically increase sales
- a particular ROI before baseline data exists
- zero employee involvement

Use:

> We would need to see the current systems and workflow before confirming the exact implementation.

---

# 17. CRM + HUMAN TEAM POSITIONING

The CRM should make employees more capable.

Examples:

The CRM remembers follow-up dates.
The salesperson has the conversation.

The CRM routes the lead.
The employee closes the customer.

AI summarizes the call.
The employee reviews and acts.

Automation sends routine reminders.
The human handles exceptions and relationships.

Reporting surfaces the problem.
Management makes the decision.

This fits the YAD doctrine:

BETTER SYSTEMS AROUND GOOD PEOPLE.
