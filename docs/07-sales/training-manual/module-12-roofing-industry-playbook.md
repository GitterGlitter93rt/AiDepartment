# MODULE 12 - ROOFING INDUSTRY PLAYBOOK

Status: Working Draft - Internal Sales Training
Vertical Priority: 3

## Purpose

This playbook teaches a YAD salesperson how roofing companies acquire leads, perform inspections, present proposals, work insurance/retail opportunities, follow up, schedule production, communicate with customers, and manage storm-driven demand.

The rep diagnoses business workflow, not roofing systems.

Core question:

> Where are paid leads, storm demand, inspections, unsold proposals, salesperson follow-up, administrative capacity, and attribution leaking between the first inquiry and collected roofing revenue?

---

# 1. WHY ROOFING IS A PRIORITY YAD VERTICAL

Roofing companies can combine:

- high-ticket jobs
- competitive local lead markets
- paid search and Meta advertising
- storm-driven surges
- phone / web lead urgency
- inspection scheduling
- field-sales teams
- estimates / proposals
- financing
- insurance-related administration
- long follow-up cycles
- CRM dependence
- large dormant lead databases
- customer-status communication
- multi-market expansion

That can create measurable YAD opportunities across growth, sales operations, CRM, employee capacity, and reporting.

# 4. LEAD SPEED

High-intent roofing prospects may contact multiple contractors.

Do not claim the first responder always wins.

Ask:

> How quickly does a brand-new roofing lead actually hear from someone during normal hours? What about after hours or during a storm surge?

Potential improvements:

- immediate acknowledgment
- lead routing
- CRM record creation
- salesperson notifications
- scheduling
- no-response follow-up
- human escalation

# 10. PUBLIC EVIDENCE CARDS

## Evidence Card A - Residential Contractor Follow-Up

ServiceTitan 2025 Residential Services Report surveyed 1,000+ contractors across HVAC, electrical, plumbing, roofing and related services.

Reported result: among contractors with annual revenue of $10M+, 47% said following up on unsold estimates resulted in 11-15% of income.

Approved wording:

> Cross-trade contractor research from ServiceTitan suggests mature residential-service companies treat unsold-estimate follow-up as a meaningful revenue discipline.

Forbidden:

> Roofing companies make 15% more if they automate follow-up.

Limitation: cross-trade vendor study, not roofing-only.

Source: https://www.servicetitan.com/press/residential-industry-report-2025

## Evidence Card B - Phone Communication

Same study: 64% of surveyed residential-service contractors said phone calls remained their dominant communication channel.

Use as directional evidence for residential-service prospecting, not a roofing-only statistic.

## Evidence Card C - Roofing Labor

U.S. BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook: median roofer wage was $55,440 in May 2025; projected employment growth 5% from 2025-2035; about 12,000 openings annually on average.

Approved wording:

> Roofing labor and operational capacity have real economic value; avoidable administrative friction deserves measurement.

Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/roofers.htm

Do not substitute national median wages for client-loaded labor cost.

# 21. ROI EXAMPLE - UNSOLD ROOFING ESTIMATES

HYPOTHETICAL ONLY.

Inputs:

- 80 proposals/month
- $15,000 average proposed job value
- 45% close today
- 44 proposals remain unsold
- 50% of unsold proposals are still realistic opportunities
- 8% incremental close scenario from improved follow-up on realistic open opportunities

Realistic active unsold opportunities = 44 x 50% = 22
Scenario additional sales = 22 x 8% = 1.76
Illustrative monthly revenue scenario = 1.76 x $15,000 = $26,400

Required language:

> This is not a promise that the company gains $26,400. It demonstrates how a small change in a high-value pipeline can matter. We need your actual open-proposal data, close rates, reasons for loss, and average sold revenue.

Use Unsold Estimate / Follow-Up ROI worksheet.

# 27. ROOFING STORIES

## The Storm Surge

HYPOTHETICAL: storm hits; ad/forms/calls spike; office cannot respond uniformly; valuable leads become stale.

Question:

> What happens here when a normal day's lead volume becomes five times larger?

## The Proposal Graveyard

HYPOTHETICAL: salesperson sends high-value proposal; prospect says think about it; rep gets new inspections; old proposal disappears.

Question:

> How close is that to your current follow-up process?

## The Marketing Black Box

HYPOTHETICAL: owner sees Google leads and accounting revenue but cannot connect which campaigns produced sold roofs.

Question:

> Can you currently connect those two systems?

---

# 28. ROOFING OBJECTIONS

## "OUR SALESPEOPLE FOLLOW UP"

> Good. How standardized is the process, and can management see whether it actually happened on every open proposal?

## "WE ALREADY HAVE A CRM"

> Great. Can it show every proposal that has gone untouched for seven days and the dollar value sitting in that stage?

## "WE HAVE AN AGENCY"

> Good. I'm not assuming the advertising is the problem. I'm interested in what happens after the agency creates the lead and whether you can tie it to a signed job.

## "STORM LEADS ARE DIFFERENT"

> They can be. That's exactly why we'd map the surge workflow rather than use a generic automation.

## "WE DON'T WANT AUTOMATED MESSAGES ANNOYING PEOPLE"

> Neither do we. The frequency, content, channel, consent, and human escalation need to match the customer relationship and applicable rules.

---

# 29. WHEN NOT TO SELL

Deprioritize if:

- lead volume is insufficient
- no measurable follow-up gap exists
- CRM/process is already strong
- proposed solution economics are weak
- company expects guaranteed sales
- required integrations are unsupported
- communication plan would be noncompliant
- leadership wants employee replacement as the primary goal

# 30. ROOFING FIELD CHEAT SHEET

## Best Signals

Active ads, storm markets, financing, multi-location, heavy sales team, high review count, open hiring, large CRM database.

## Top Five Hooks

1. Unsold proposals
2. Paid-lead response
3. Storm surge
4. Marketing attribution
5. Sales/admin capacity

## Best First Question

> What happens to a roofing proposal that does not sign during the first conversation?

## Financial Follow-Up

> How many are sitting open, what is a typical sold job worth, and how many are still legitimate opportunities?

## ROI Tool

Unsold Estimate / Follow-Up ROI.

## Employee-Safe Position

> The goal is to let salespeople spend more time selling and the office spend more time helping customers while routine tracking and reminders become more consistent.

# MODULE 12 CORE RULE

Roofing sales economics can be highly sensitive to what happens after the lead and after the proposal. YAD should help the contractor measure those handoffs, strengthen the system around the sales team, and make high-value opportunity visible - without pretending every open estimate is recoverable revenue.
