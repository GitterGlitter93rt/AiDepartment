# MODULE 39 - ROLEPLAY ACADEMY

Status: Working Draft - Internal Sales Training

## Purpose

The Your AI Department sales system cannot be learned by reading alone.

A salesperson must be able to perform the system under pressure:

- on the phone
- in person
- with a gatekeeper
- with a skeptical owner
- with an executive
- when the prospect has no obvious pain
- when the prospect has strong pain but weak data
- when the prospect challenges AI, price, trust, employees, integrations, or ROI

The Roleplay Academy converts the manual into observable sales behavior.

Core training cycle:

LEARN
->
WATCH
->
PRACTICE
->
RECORD
->
SCORE
->
COACH
->
REPEAT
->
CERTIFY

---

# 1. ROLEPLAY RULES

Roleplay should feel like a real business conversation, not a memorization contest.

The rep is graded on judgment and process.

Do not reward:

- talking fastest
- using the most AI terminology
- overpowering objections
- forcing every prospect into a meeting
- inventing numbers
- pretending a capability is confirmed

Reward:

- relevance
- listening
- useful questions
- financial diagnosis
- claim discipline
- employee-safe language
- technical humility
- clear next steps
- knowing when to disqualify

---

# 2. FOUR TRAINING LEVELS

## LEVEL 1 - FOUNDATIONS

Rep practices one skill at a time.

Examples:

- 20-second opener
- gatekeeper response
- one hook
- one discovery sequence
- one objection
- one ROI explanation

## LEVEL 2 - COMBINED CONVERSATION

Rep handles a 5-10 minute conversation with multiple skills.

## LEVEL 3 - ADVERSARIAL / COMPLEX

Prospect is skeptical, busy, unclear, or gives conflicting information.

## LEVEL 4 - EXECUTIVE / FIELD CERTIFICATION

Rep must diagnose a business, quantify responsibly, select the right next step, and protect YAD from overpromising.

# 4. IN-PERSON / WALK-IN ROLEPLAYS

Every rep must practice:

- front-desk introduction
- receptionist asks "what are you selling?"
- owner is not available
- owner unexpectedly walks out
- owner gives 30 seconds
- owner gives three minutes
- employee becomes defensive about AI
- "leave something"
- owner says return another day
- owner wants to see a demo immediately
- owner asks price before discovery
- busy shop / contractor office
- formal professional office
- healthcare / law / financial office with privacy constraints

The rep is graded on body language and exit behavior as well as words.

---

# 5. OPENING DRILL

Manager gives:

- company
- industry
- city
- one public signal

Rep has 15 seconds to choose a hook.

Example:

Company: HVAC contractor
Signal: active Google Ads for AC replacement + 24/7 service

Rep:

> I noticed you advertise around AC replacement and emergency service. When one of those paid calls comes in after hours and nobody gets to it immediately, what happens next?

Manager then changes the signal.

Goal: hook selection becomes instinctive.

---

# 6. TWO-MINUTE RESEARCH DRILL

Give the rep a public company profile / website snapshot.

Rep must identify:

- business model
- primary customer value
- likely lead sources
- active advertiser signal if visible
- probable workflow pressure point
- primary hook
- backup hook
- decision-maker role
- which ROI worksheet might become relevant

The rep must distinguish:

PUBLIC FACT
from
HYPOTHESIS.

---

# 7. DISCOVERY DRILL

Manager provides vague pain:

> We miss some calls.

Rep must turn it into:

- frequency
- legitimate-opportunity percentage
- customer value
- current recovery process
- existing software
- desired outcome
- missing data

The rep fails if they immediately pitch an AI phone agent.

---

# 8. WORKFLOW MAPPING DRILL

Manager gives a process:

> A lead submits a website form.

Rep draws / verbally maps:

TRIGGER
->
INPUT
->
SYSTEM
->
OWNER
->
ACTION
->
HANDOFF
->
OUTCOME
->
MEASUREMENT

At each handoff the rep asks:

- Who owns this?
- What system?
- How long?
- What fails?
- How often?
- Is it measured?

---

# 9. ROI ROLEPLAY

Manager gives partial numbers.

Example:

- 500 calls/month
- prospect thinks 10% missed
- $900 average customer
- does not know legitimate-opportunity percentage

Correct rep behavior:

- calculate only what is supported
- label client estimate
- mark missing data
- avoid pretending all missed calls equal revenue
- create a conservative scenario only if assumptions are clearly labeled

Required phrase:

> This is an illustrative scenario, not a guarantee. We should replace this assumption with your actual data.

---

# 10. EMPLOYEE-SAFE ROLEPLAY

Prospect:

> So this means I can fire my receptionist?

Passing response:

> Workforce decisions are yours, but that isn't where we start. We'd first measure what the front office is handling, which work is repetitive, where volume exceeds capacity, and what should stay human. The goal is better capacity and customer response around the team you already have.

Hard fail:

> Absolutely - AI is cheaper than her.

---

# 11. TECHNICAL LIMITATION DRILL

Prospect:

> Can you integrate with our system?

Rep must ask:

- What system?
- What data/action?
- Is there an API/native integration?
- What permissions/security?

Passing response:

> Possibly, but I don't want to promise it until our technical team verifies the system and exact workflow.

Hard fail:

> We can integrate anything.

---

# 12. NO-SALE ROLEPLAY

Manager creates a prospect with:

- low volume
- low customer value
- strong existing process
- no measurable pain

Rep passes by saying:

> Based on what you've shown me, I don't think there's a strong enough business case to force a project right now.

The rep fails if they manufacture pain.

---

# 14. HVAC SCENARIO

Public facts:

- two locations
- 24/7 emergency service
- active Google Ads
- replacement financing

Hidden prospect truth:

- after-hours answering service exists
- CRM follow-up on replacement estimates is weak

Rep should abandon the missed-call hypothesis after learning answering is strong and pivot to estimate follow-up / attribution.

---

# 15. PLUMBING SCENARIO

Public facts:

- active emergency-plumber ads
- six trucks
- strong reviews

Hidden truth:

- calls are answered well
- dispatcher manually copies web leads into field-service software
- larger sewer estimates have no standard follow-up

Rep must discover instead of forcing the original hook.

---

# 16. ROOFING SCENARIO

Public facts:

- storm market
- Meta and Google campaigns
- 12 salespeople

Hidden truth:

- proposals live in CRM
- follow-up is rep-dependent
- management cannot see stale proposal value

Rep should move to Unsold Estimate ROI.

---

# 17. COLLISION SCENARIO

Public facts:

- high-volume shop
- active search ads
- busy front office

Hidden truth:

- missed estimate calls are moderate
- customer status calls consume major CSR time
- shop-management status data is inconsistent

Correct recommendation may begin with workflow/data cleanup before customer automation.

---

# 18. PDR/HAIL SCENARIO

Public facts:

- temporary hail market
- six field sales reps
- Meta campaign

Hidden truth:

- leads live in rep phones
- appointments tracked in spreadsheet
- management cannot measure source-to-repair

Rep should diagnose CRM / field lead capture before asking for more advertising.

---

# 19. LAW FIRM SCENARIO

Public facts:

- PI firm
- heavy Google Ads
- intake hours end at 6 PM

Hidden truth:

- partner is concerned about confidentiality
- firm uses a legal CRM
- employee ChatGPT use is uncontrolled

Rep should preserve legal judgment, discuss intake/admin workflows, and recognize governance/training as an opportunity.

---

# 20. OBJECTION LIGHTNING ROUND

Manager reads one objection every 20-30 seconds.

Rep has to:

1. clarify when necessary
2. answer concisely
3. ask a next question

Do not allow 3-minute monologues.

---

# 21. STORYTELLING DRILL

Rep must tell the same scenario in:

- 15 seconds
- 45 seconds
- two minutes

Then immediately ask a relevance question.

Required labels:

REAL CASE STUDY - VERIFIED
or
HYPOTHETICAL BUSINESS SCENARIO.

Invented case studies = hard fail.

---

# 22. WHITEBOARD DRILL

Rep gets one marker and must draw:

LEAD
->
RESPONSE
->
FOLLOW-UP
->
SALE
->
REVENUE

Then adapt the diagram to the prospect's actual workflow.

Scoring rewards clarity, not artistic ability.

---

# 23. PROPOSAL PRESENTATION ROLEPLAY

Rep must present:

- confirmed problem
- current workflow
- business impact
- future-state workflow
- assumptions
- scope
- exclusions
- measurement
- investment
- next decision

The proposal should not become a feature dump.

---

# 24. EXECUTIVE CHALLENGE ROLEPLAY

CFO / CEO persona asks:

- Why now?
- Why YAD?
- What if assumptions are wrong?
- Why not buy software?
- How will this be measured?
- Who owns implementation?
- What is the downside?
- What happens if employees do not adopt it?

Rep must answer without hype.

# 25. ROLEPLAY SCORECARD - 100 POINTS

## Opening / Relevance - 10

- concise
- relevant
- business-first

## Listening / Conversation Control - 10

- does not interrupt
- follows prospect answers

## Discovery - 15

- workflow
- frequency
- owner
- failure point

## Economics / Quantification - 15

- asks volume/value
- labels assumptions
- conservative math

## YAD Positioning - 10

- outcomes not jargon
- correct capability/status language

## Employee-Safe Language - 10

- capacity framing
- no replacement pitch

## Objection Handling - 10

- clarify/respond/next question

## Technical / Risk Discipline - 10

- no unsupported integration/security/compliance promise

## Next Step / Close - 5

- logical commitment

## CRM / Notes / Follow-Up - 5

- accurate outcome and prospect language

---

# 26. HARD-FAIL EVENTS

Regardless of numeric score, the roleplay fails if the rep:

- invents a statistic
- invents a client case/result
- guarantees revenue/ROI
- claims all theoretical exposure is recoverable
- promises an unverified integration
- recommends firing/replacing employees as the pitch
- gives legal/medical/financial/insurance advice
- recommends unsafe autonomous decision-making
- lies to a gatekeeper
- invents pricing/discount/payment terms
- ignores a clear do-not-contact / leave request

---

# 27. RECORDING AND REVIEW

Where legally and operationally appropriate, practice roleplays should be recorded for coaching.

Review method:

1. Rep self-scores first.
2. Manager scores second.
3. Compare differences.
4. Select one or two behaviors to improve.
5. Repeat scenario.

Do not overwhelm the rep with 25 corrections at once.

# 29. REMEDIATION

If rep fails:

Do not simply say:

> Practice more.

Assign the failed competency.

Example:

Failed: economics
Remediation: Module 2A + 2B + five ROI drills.

Failed: employee language
Remediation: Module 1 + employee objection drills.

Failed: gatekeeper
Remediation: Module 4A/4B + ten gatekeeper repetitions.

Failed: technical promises
Remediation: Modules 1, 8, 9 + technical limitation drills.

---

# 30. PASS STANDARD

Recommended working standard:

- 85/100 or better
- no hard-fail event
- manager confirms conversation sounds natural rather than memorized

Final company certification policy may adjust the numeric threshold.

# MODULE 39 CORE RULE

A salesperson is not trained because they read the manual.

They are trained when they can apply it under pressure, protect the company's credibility, and make good business judgments without a script in front of them.
