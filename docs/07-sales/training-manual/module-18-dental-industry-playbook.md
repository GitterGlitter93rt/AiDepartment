# MODULE 18 - DENTAL INDUSTRY PLAYBOOK

Status: Working Draft - Internal Sales Training
Vertical Priority: 9
Evidence Status: Core workflow approved; ADA HPI economic figures must be publication-verified before final PDF.

## Purpose

Teach YAD salespeople how dental practices acquire patients, handle new-patient inquiries, schedule appointments, manage no-shows, present treatment, follow up unscheduled treatment, reactivate patients, use front-desk capacity, and measure marketing while protecting patient privacy and clinical judgment.

The rep diagnoses business workflow, not dental treatment.

Core question:

> Where are new-patient opportunities, scheduled chair time, treatment follow-up, front-desk capacity, reactivation, and marketing visibility leaking between first inquiry and completed treatment?

- restorative dentistry
- crowns / bridges
- implants
- cosmetic dentistry

---

# 3. PUBLIC ECONOMIC CONTEXT

Prior ADA Health Policy Institute research captured for this project reported average annual gross billings around $965,660 for private-practice general dentists and around $1.21M for specialists in the cited dataset, with expenses rising faster than reimbursement in recent reporting.

Before final publication, verify the exact ADA HPI table, data year, geography, practice definition, and publication date.

Approved use after verification:

> Dental practices operate meaningful revenue-producing schedules, so missed appointments, weak treatment follow-up, and front-office inefficiency can be economically important.

Forbidden:

> The average dentist makes $X, therefore your practice is losing $Y.

Always use the practice's own production/collection data for ROI.

# 10. FRONT-DESK CAPACITY

Look for:

- repetitive calls
- reminder messages
- rescheduling
- insurance/admin questions
- CRM/PMS notes
- treatment follow-up
- recall calls
- review requests
- report assembly

Ask:

Employee-safe framing: reduce repetitive work; increase patient-facing capacity.

# 17. WALK-IN

Dental offices require privacy awareness.

> Hi, I'm [Rep] with Your AI Department. I don't have an appointment. We work with practices on new-patient response, scheduling, no-shows, treatment follow-up, front-desk capacity, CRM, and marketing attribution. Who handles business operations or growth here?

Never inspect patient screens, paperwork, or clinical areas.

---

# 18. OBJECTIONS

## "PATIENTS WANT A PERSON"

> They should have a person for clinical and relationship-driven conversations. We're looking at routine intake, reminders, scheduling, CRM updates, and other repetitive work around the team.

## "OUR SOFTWARE ALREADY DOES REMINDERS"

> Great. Then we leave that alone. What part of new-patient follow-up, unscheduled treatment, attribution, or reporting is still manual?

## "WE CAN'T PUT PATIENT DATA INTO AI"

> We shouldn't put sensitive data into an unapproved tool. Any workflow involving patient data needs technical/privacy review before implementation.

# 19. ROI EXAMPLE - NO-SHOWS

HYPOTHETICAL:

- 1,000 appointments/month
- 5% no-show / unrecovered late-cancel exposure selected for scenario
- $180 average schedule production/value metric
- 20% realistically recoverable through better confirmation/rescheduling/fill workflow

Exposed appointments = 50
Scenario recovered = 10
Illustrative schedule value = $1,800/month

This is not guaranteed revenue and should use the practice's own appointment categories and production metrics.

# 20. FIELD CHEAT SHEET

Best signals: implant/cosmetic ads, multi-location, heavy new-patient marketing, online booking, active hiring, high review volume.

Top hooks: missed new-patient calls; no-shows; unscheduled treatment; front-desk capacity; attribution.

Best first question:

> What happens to a new-patient inquiry when the front desk can't respond immediately?

ROI tools: Lead Response, No-Show, Employee Capacity.

Employee-safe: better systems around front desk and treatment coordinators; clinical decisions stay human.

Dental opportunity often lives in schedule utilization, patient follow-up, and front-desk capacity. YAD can improve the business system around care, but qualified dental professionals remain responsible for clinical decisions and patient treatment.
