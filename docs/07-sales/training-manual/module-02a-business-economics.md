# Module 2A - Business Economics for YAD Salespeople

Status: Approved Working Draft

## Purpose

A Your AI Department salesperson must understand enough business economics to connect operational problems to money, time, employee capacity, customers, and investment return.

The goal is not to produce the largest possible number. The goal is to build a credible business case.

## Financial diagnosis model

Exposure -> Addressable Opportunity -> Realistically Recoverable Opportunity -> Business Value -> Investment -> ROI / Payback

## Core rules

- Exposure is not recoverable revenue.
- Revenue is not profit.
- Time saved is not automatically payroll savings.
- Employee capacity is not employee replacement.
- Client data is preferable to external benchmarks where reliable data exists.
- Assumptions must be labeled.
- Missing data should be identified rather than invented.
- Use conservative, expected, and aggressive scenarios when uncertainty is meaningful.
- Attack the workflow, not the employee.

## Four number labels

1. Verified Client Data
2. Client-Provided Estimate
3. External Benchmark
4. Illustrative / Planning Assumption

Salespeople must know which category every important number belongs to.

## Five numbers to look for

1. Volume - how many times does this happen?
2. Value - what is each event/customer/job worth?
3. Failure Rate - how much falls through the crack?
4. Realistic Improvement - what portion could reasonably improve?
5. Cost to Fix - what does the solution cost?

## Core concepts

Salespeople should be comfortable discussing:
- revenue
- gross profit / contribution
- transaction value
- customer lifetime value
- lead value
- lead/opportunity/appointment/customer stages
- conversion rate
- cost per lead
- customer acquisition cost
- attribution
- capacity
- loaded labor cost
- overtime
- avoided/deferred hiring
- sales cycle
- pipeline value
- reactivation economics
- no-show economics
- working-capital improvement
- hard ROI vs operational/strategic value
- ROI
- payback period
- cost of delay

## The three-number YAD presentation

For material revenue opportunities, present:

1. Theoretical Exposure
2. Realistically Recoverable Scenario
3. Potential ROI / Payback

Do not collapse these into one inflated number.

## Example

300 opportunities per month x 20% unanswered x $850 average job = $51,000 monthly theoretical exposure.

If 65% of unanswered contacts appear to be legitimate opportunities and a 30% capture/close scenario is used:

60 unanswered x 65% legitimate x 30% scenario capture x $850 ~= $9,945-$10,200 monthly recoverable revenue scenario depending rounding.

The $51,000 is not a promise. The second figure is still a scenario, not a guarantee.

## Employee capacity

Recovered employee time should normally be described as capacity value, not payroll savings.

Potential value may come from:
- serving more customers
- selling more
- reducing overtime
- completing work faster
- improving customer experience
- reducing backlog
- delaying an unnecessary future hire
- redirecting employees toward higher-value work

Preferred language:
"Potential annual labor capacity value."

Avoid:
"Guaranteed payroll savings."

## Strong financial language

- "Let's use your numbers."
- "I'd rather calculate this conservatively."
- "That's theoretical exposure, not guaranteed revenue."
- "Let's separate total opportunity from what may realistically be recoverable."
- "I don't want to call that wasted spend until we audit it."
- "That represents employee capacity, not automatic payroll savings."
- "We need more data before I'd put an ROI number on that."
- "If the conservative case doesn't justify the investment, we shouldn't force it."

## Avoid

- "You're definitely losing $100,000."
- "This will make you $500,000."
- "We'll double your close rate."
- "We can save you three employees."
- "You can fire your receptionist."
- "This guarantees ROI."

## Final rule

Never ask, "How big can I make this number?"

Ask, "What is the smallest number I can defend?"
