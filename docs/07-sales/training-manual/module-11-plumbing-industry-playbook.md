# MODULE 11 - PLUMBING INDUSTRY PLAYBOOK

Status: Working Draft - Internal Sales Training
Vertical Priority: 2

## Purpose

This playbook teaches a Your AI Department salesperson how to have an intelligent business conversation with a residential or light-commercial plumbing contractor without pretending to be a plumber.

The salesperson diagnoses business workflow, not plumbing systems.

Core question:

> Where are urgent customer demand, paid leads, technician capacity, follow-up, and management visibility leaking between the first call and collected revenue?

---

# 1. WHY PLUMBING IS A PRIORITY YAD VERTICAL

Plumbing is attractive because many contractors combine:

- urgent inbound calls
- nights/weekends/on-call work
- strong Google search intent
- phone-dependent booking
- dispatch complexity
- skilled technicians whose time is valuable
- estimates for larger work
- financing on selected jobs
- recurring / membership opportunities
- repetitive office communication
- CRM / field-service platforms
- review-driven local buying
- old customer databases
- multi-location growth for larger operators

Do not assume every plumber has every problem. Use these as hypotheses.

# 4. THE URGENCY ADVANTAGE AND RISK

A homeowner with active water damage, no hot water, a backed-up drain, or another urgent issue often wants help now.

That can make response speed commercially important.

Do not claim every customer chooses the first responder.

Ask:

> When somebody calls with an urgent plumbing problem and the office cannot answer immediately, what happens next?

---

# 5. PHONE HANDLING

Potential failure points:

- calls ring unanswered
- overflow reaches voicemail
- after-hours inquiries wait until morning
- missed calls are not tracked
- no automatic recovery task exists
- callback depends on employee memory
- new-customer calls are mixed with vendor/existing-customer traffic

Potential YAD capabilities:

- intelligent routing
- missed-call workflow
- immediate SMS acknowledgment
- AI-assisted phone handling where appropriate
- basic intake
- scheduling where supported
- CRM / field-service record creation
- call summaries
- human escalation

Never claim all missed calls are new customers.

# 6. DISPATCH AND SCHEDULING

Plumbing businesses may deal with:

- emergency vs non-emergency prioritization
- technician skill matching
- geographic routing
- rescheduling
- cancellations
- customer ETA communication
- job extensions
- technician availability

YAD should not promise autonomous safety-critical dispatch decisions without understanding the platform, rules, and human oversight.

Potential opportunity:

> Can routine scheduling, confirmations, reminders, and customer updates become more consistent while dispatchers retain control over exceptions and priorities?

# 9. CRM / FIELD-SERVICE SYSTEM

A plumbing company may already use a field-service platform or CRM.

The question is not just whether software exists.

Ask:

- Does every new inquiry enter the system?
- Is the source captured?
- Is the lead/job assigned correctly?
- What happens after no contact?
- Are estimates tracked?
- Can management see untouched opportunities?
- Are calls, texts, and emails visible?
- Can revenue be tied back to source?
- Are old customers segmented for reactivation?

YAD may help evaluate, configure, integrate, automate, report, and train around the appropriate platform.

Never promise an integration before technical review.

# 14. EMPLOYEE CAPACITY

Skilled plumbers and strong dispatch/front-office employees are expensive resources.

Look for time consumed by:

- duplicate entry
- manual CRM updates
- repetitive texts
- status calls
- report assembly
- copying data between systems
- call summaries
- follow-up reminders
- customer FAQs

Ask:

> What administrative work keeps good employees busy that does not require their judgment?

> If you gave the dispatcher five hours back every week, where would you want those hours spent?

Use capacity language, not employee-replacement language.

# 15. PUBLIC EVIDENCE CARDS

## Evidence Card A - Residential Services Communication

Source: ServiceTitan 2025 Residential Services Report / press release.
Population: 1,000+ residential service contractors in HVAC, electrical, plumbing, roofing, and related sectors across the United States.
Claim: 64% of surveyed contractors reported phone calls as their dominant communication channel.
Approved wording:

> A 2025 ServiceTitan survey of more than 1,000 residential service contractors found phone calls remained the dominant communication channel for 64% of respondents.

Limitation: not plumbing-only; vendor research.

Source URL: https://www.servicetitan.com/press/residential-industry-report-2025

## Evidence Card B - Estimate Follow-Up

Same ServiceTitan study.
Claim: 47% of contractors with $10M+ annual revenue said follow-up on unsold estimates accounted for 11-15% of income.
Approved wording:

> In ServiceTitan's cross-trade survey, nearly half of contractors above $10M in annual revenue reported meaningful income associated with unsold-estimate follow-up.

Forbidden overgeneralization:

> Following up will increase your plumbing revenue by 15%.

## Evidence Card C - Skilled Labor Economics

Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook.
Data: median annual wage for plumbers, pipefitters and steamfitters was $63,800 in May 2025; projected employment growth 7% from 2025-2035; about 42,000 openings annually on average.
Approved wording:

> Skilled plumbing labor has meaningful economic value, making technician and staff capacity worth protecting.

Source URL: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/plumbers-pipefitters-and-steamfitters.htm

Do not convert national wage data directly into a client's loaded labor cost. Ask the company for its actual compensation data.

# 19. ROI EXAMPLE - MISSED PLUMBING CALLS

HYPOTHETICAL ONLY.

Assume client-provided / illustrative inputs:

- 1,200 monthly inbound calls
- 8% unanswered
- 45% of unanswered calls are legitimate new opportunities
- 30% scenario capture/close rate on qualified missed opportunities
- $650 average customer revenue

Unanswered = 1,200 x 8% = 96
Qualified missed opportunities = 96 x 45% = 43.2
Scenario customers = 43.2 x 30% = 12.96
Illustrative recoverable revenue scenario = 12.96 x $650 = $8,424/month

Required rep language:

> This is an illustrative scenario, not a promise. We would replace these assumptions with your call logs, opportunity mix, close rates, and customer value.

Use Missed Call ROI worksheet.

# 20. ROI EXAMPLE - DISPATCH CAPACITY

HYPOTHETICAL ONLY.

Assume:

- 3 office employees
- 5 repetitive admin hours/week each
- 48 productive weeks
- 50% of that workload realistically addressable
- $32 estimated loaded labor cost/hour

Annual repetitive hours = 3 x 5 x 48 = 720
Addressable hours = 720 x 50% = 360
Potential annual capacity value = 360 x $32 = $11,520

Do not call this guaranteed payroll savings.

Ask:

> What could the team do with those recovered hours?

Use Employee Capacity ROI worksheet.

# 29. PLUMBING-SPECIFIC OBJECTIONS

## "OUR ANSWERING SERVICE HANDLES AFTER HOURS"

> Good. I wouldn't replace something that's working. Does it create a structured lead in your system, capture the right information, and give management visibility into response and booking?

## "WE HAVE SERVICETITAN / HOUSECALL PRO / JOBBER / OTHER SOFTWARE"

> Good. The question isn't whether you own software. It's whether the workflow is configured around the way you actually sell and service customers. What is the biggest thing you wish the system did better?

Do not criticize a platform you have not reviewed.

## "OUR DISPATCHERS ALREADY DO THIS"

> They probably do. We're looking for the repetitive parts around their judgment that could be handled more consistently so they have more capacity for customers and exceptions.

## "MOST OF OUR BUSINESS IS REFERRALS"

> That's valuable. How consistently do those referral calls get captured, followed up, and attributed today?

## "WE DON'T NEED AI"

> You may not. The better question is whether you have any expensive workflow problem worth fixing. The answer could be CRM configuration, ordinary automation, integration, or nothing at all.

# 30. WHEN NOT TO SELL

Deprioritize when:

- call / lead volume is very low
- customer values are too small to support the project
- current workflow is already strong and measured
- no decision-maker will participate
- owner demands guaranteed results
- company primarily wants to fire staff
- required integrations are not feasible
- company refuses necessary security / human controls

# MODULE 11 CORE RULE

Plumbing customers often have urgent problems and contractors often pay heavily for the opportunity to answer them.

The YAD rep's job is to determine whether the contractor's business system reliably turns that demand into a tracked, serviced, paid, and retained customer - while helping good employees handle more volume rather than making them the problem.
