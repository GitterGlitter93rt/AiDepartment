# MODULE 15 - LAW FIRMS INDUSTRY PLAYBOOK

Status: Working Draft - Internal Sales Training
Vertical Priority: 6

## Purpose

This playbook teaches YAD salespeople how law firms generate potential-client inquiries, handle intake, schedule consultations, follow up, manage documents and internal knowledge, use employees, measure marketing, and evaluate AI opportunities while preserving professional judgment, confidentiality, and required human oversight.

The YAD salesperson diagnoses business workflow, not legal matters.

Core question:

> Where are qualified inquiries, intake capacity, follow-up, administrative time, client communication, and marketing attribution leaking between the first contact and the firm's business outcome - and what should remain firmly under attorney or qualified staff control?

---

# 1. WHY LAW FIRMS ARE A PRIORITY YAD VERTICAL

Law firms may combine:

- expensive paid-search acquisition
- high-value potential matters
- phone / email / form intake
- after-hours inquiries
- intake qualification
- consultation scheduling
- missed-call risk
- document-heavy workflows
- repetitive administrative work
- CRM / practice-management systems
- confidential client information
- marketing attribution gaps
- internal knowledge needs
- significant professional-risk constraints

This can create major value from better systems, but legal is a higher-risk vertical than ordinary home services.

YAD must be conservative around claims, data, legal advice, case evaluation, confidentiality, and automation authority.

# 5. PUBLIC EVIDENCE CARD - CLIENT RESPONSIVENESS

Source: Clio 2024 Legal Trends Report / 2024 secret-shop study.

Study design: third-party research company contacted 500 law firms via phone and email.

Reported findings:

- 40% answered phone calls
- 52% either answered or returned the phone call
- 48% were effectively unreachable by phone
- 33% responded to emails

Approved wording:

> In Clio's 2024 secret-shop study of 500 law firms, response gaps were substantial: only 40% answered calls, and 33% responded to email inquiries.

Use this to justify investigating intake responsiveness, not to claim a particular prospect has the same problem.

Sources:
https://www.clio.com/about/press/clio-latest-legal-trends-report/
https://www.clio.com/guides/client-intake-legal-trends/

Limitation: vendor-sponsored legal-industry research; individual firms may perform much better.

# 6. PUBLIC EVIDENCE CARD - CLIENT-FACING TECHNOLOGY

Clio's 2024 report associated use of certain client-facing intake technologies with higher lead and revenue outcomes in its customer / research dataset.

Approved wording:

> Clio's research suggests firms using client-facing intake technologies can show materially different business outcomes, which makes intake technology worth evaluating.

Do NOT say:

> Installing our intake system will increase your revenue by 52%.

Correlation / vendor-research findings are not a guaranteed causal result for a new client.

# 7. PROFESSIONAL-ETHICS / AI EVIDENCE CARD

Source: American Bar Association Formal Opinion 512, July 29, 2024.

ABA guidance identifies ethical obligations relevant to lawyers using generative AI, including competence, confidentiality, communication, supervision, candor, meritorious claims, and reasonable fees.

Approved YAD training conclusion:

> AI implementation in a law firm requires governance, tool evaluation, confidentiality safeguards, appropriate supervision, and attorney accountability; it is not simply a productivity-software deployment.

Source:
https://www.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba/administrative/professional_responsibility/ethics-opinions/aba-formal-opinion-512.pdf

Important: state bar rules, court rules, client requirements, and jurisdiction-specific obligations may impose additional or different requirements. YAD does not provide legal ethics advice.

# 23. ROI EXAMPLE - INTAKE RESPONSE

HYPOTHETICAL ONLY.

Assume:

- 300 monthly new inquiries
- 15% are not successfully contacted within the firm's target response process
- 35% of those are legitimately qualified for deeper intake
- 12% scenario incremental retention from improving response/follow-up on those qualified missed opportunities
- $4,000 illustrative average first-year collected revenue/value metric selected by firm

Unsuccessfully contacted = 300 x 15% = 45
Qualified = 45 x 35% = 15.75
Scenario additional retained matters = 15.75 x 12% = 1.89
Illustrative scenario value = 1.89 x $4,000 = $7,560/month equivalent

This is an illustrative model only.

For contingency firms, matter economics can be delayed, variable, and highly skewed; use the firm's chosen defensible value metric and do not promise case outcomes.

# 24. ROI EXAMPLE - INTAKE CAPACITY

HYPOTHETICAL.

- 4 intake/admin employees
- 4 repetitive hours/week each
- 48 weeks
- 40% realistically addressable
- $38 loaded hourly labor cost

Annual repetitive hours = 4 x 4 x 48 = 768
Addressable hours = 768 x 40% = 307.2
Potential annual capacity value = 307.2 x $38 = $11,673.60

This is capacity value, not automatic payroll savings.

# 31. LAW-FIRM STORIES

## The 9:30 PM Inquiry

HYPOTHETICAL: potential client contacts multiple firms; Firm A responds tomorrow; Firm B acknowledges, collects basic approved intake information, and schedules appropriate follow-up.

Question:

> What happens to that inquiry at your firm?

## The Repeated Intake

HYPOTHETICAL: prospect fills form, tells receptionist the same facts, then repeats them during consultation because systems did not carry the information cleanly.

Question:

> Where is information being collected more than once today?

## The Marketing Black Box

HYPOTHETICAL: agency reports leads; intake reports consultations; accounting reports revenue; nobody connects them.

Question:

> Can you tie those stages together today?

## The Uncontrolled AI User

HYPOTHETICAL: an employee uses a public AI tool to summarize work without clear firm policy, tool approval, or data-handling rules.

Question:

> Do you know which AI tools employees are already using and what information goes into them?

# 32. LAW-FIRM OBJECTIONS

## "CLIENTS WANT A LAWYER, NOT AI"

> They should get a lawyer where legal judgment or advice is required. We're looking at the administrative workflow around the lawyer - intake capture, scheduling, reminders, summaries, CRM updates, document organization, and reporting.

## "CONFIDENTIALITY MAKES THIS IMPOSSIBLE"

> Confidentiality makes tool selection and workflow design more demanding, not something we ignore. We need to understand the data, vendor terms, permissions, retention, and human-review requirements before recommending a system.

## "WE ALREADY HAVE CLIO / FILEVINE / LAWYAW / OTHER PLATFORM"

> Good. If the system works well, we keep it. The question is whether the intake, communications, follow-up, attribution, and reporting are actually configured around your process.

## "WE ALREADY USE CHATGPT"

> That's exactly why governance may matter. Which version/tools are approved, what data can employees enter, and what verification rules exist?

## "WE HAVE A MARKETING AGENCY"

> Good. I'm not assuming the agency is the problem. Can you trace the agency's lead through retained-client status and the firm's chosen economic metric?

## "WE CAN'T LET AI QUALIFY CASES"

> Then we do not let it make that judgment. It can collect structured information and route it to qualified humans using criteria the firm defines.

# 33. WHEN NOT TO SELL

Stop / deprioritize when:

- no meaningful workflow problem exists
- firm expects YAD to provide legal advice
- firm wants unsupervised AI legal decisions
- confidential data cannot be protected appropriately
- required vendor/integration cannot meet firm requirements
- economics are weak
- leadership will not participate in governance/adoption
- firm demands guaranteed client acquisition or case outcomes
- marketing / outreach plan would violate applicable professional rules

# MODULE 15 CORE RULE

Law firms can gain significant value from better intake, systems, employee capacity, marketing measurement, and carefully governed AI. YAD's credibility depends on preserving the line between improving the business system and practicing law. We automate and support what is appropriate; attorneys and qualified professionals retain responsibility for legal judgment, confidentiality, ethics, and client outcomes.
