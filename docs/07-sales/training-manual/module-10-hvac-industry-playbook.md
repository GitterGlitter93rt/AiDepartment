# MODULE 10 - HVAC INDUSTRY PLAYBOOK

Status: Working Draft - Internal Sales Training
Vertical Priority: 1

## Purpose

This is the first full Your AI Department vertical playbook.

The HVAC chapter is designed to teach a salesperson enough about how an HVAC company makes money, acquires customers, handles calls, schedules work, sells replacement systems, manages follow-up, uses employees, and measures performance to conduct an intelligent business conversation without pretending to be an HVAC technician.

The salesperson's job is not to diagnose equipment.

The salesperson's job is to diagnose business workflow.

The core HVAC sales question is:

> Where are demand, customer opportunities, employee capacity, and management visibility leaking between the moment a homeowner needs help and the moment the company gets paid and retains the customer?

---

# 1. WHY HVAC IS A PRIORITY YAD VERTICAL

HVAC is attractive for Your AI Department because many contractors combine several characteristics that create measurable workflow leverage:

- urgent inbound demand
- heavy phone dependence
- high-value repair and replacement opportunities
- after-hours and weekend demand
- seasonal volume spikes
- paid-search competition
- estimate / proposal follow-up
- scheduling and dispatch complexity
- recurring maintenance relationships
- significant front-office workload
- technician labor constraints
- CRM / field-service software
- financing workflows
- customer-status communication
- reviews and local search
- multi-location growth for larger operators

A strong HVAC contractor may therefore have YAD opportunities across both growth and operations.

Do not assume every contractor has every problem.

Use the industry model to form hypotheses, then verify them with the prospect.

# 2. HVAC BUSINESS MODEL - HOW THE COMPANY MAKES MONEY

Residential HVAC companies may generate revenue from several categories.

## SERVICE AND REPAIR

Examples:

- no-cool / no-heat service calls
- diagnostics
- component repair
- refrigerant-related service
- electrical / control repair
- airflow issues
- emergency service

This work can be highly time-sensitive because the homeowner usually has an immediate comfort problem.

## REPLACEMENT / INSTALLATION

Examples:

- system replacement
- new equipment installation
- heat pump replacement
- furnace / air-handler / condenser replacement
- duct or system upgrades

Replacement opportunities are generally higher-value than ordinary service calls and often involve estimates, financing, multiple options, salesperson / comfort-advisor involvement, and follow-up.

Do not quote a universal average replacement ticket.

Actual price varies materially by geography, equipment, efficiency, size, scope, labor, incentives, and company positioning.

Ask the contractor for their real average sold replacement value.

## MAINTENANCE / MEMBERSHIPS / SERVICE AGREEMENTS

Potential revenue and strategic value include:

- seasonal maintenance
- recurring service
- priority scheduling
- member discounts
- equipment relationship continuity
- future replacement opportunity

The agreement may be valuable even if its immediate margin is modest because it can deepen the customer relationship.

## ACCESSORIES / ADD-ONS

Depending on the contractor:

- indoor air quality
- filtration
- thermostats / controls
- ductwork
- dehumidification
- zoning
- related comfort products

## COMMERCIAL WORK

Some HVAC contractors also perform commercial service, maintenance, projects, or building-system work.

This playbook primarily uses residential service-company examples because phone response, paid search, dispatch, estimates, and homeowner communication make the workflow especially clear.

For commercial-heavy contractors, discovery must be adapted to contracts, facilities teams, bid cycles, preventive maintenance, and account management.

# 4. CURRENT INDUSTRY EVIDENCE

These facts may be used in training and sales conversations only with the stated limitations.

## EVIDENCE A - PHONE REMAINS IMPORTANT

ServiceTitan's 2025 Residential Services Report surveyed more than 1,000 residential service contractors across HVAC, electrical, plumbing, roofing, and other trades.

The report stated that 64% of contractors relied on phone calls as the dominant form of customer communication / initial contact.

Approved wording:

> In a 2025 ServiceTitan survey of more than 1,000 residential service contractors across several trades, phone calls were still the dominant customer-contact channel for 64% of respondents.

Do NOT say:

> 64% of every HVAC lead comes by phone.

Limitation:

The sample spans multiple residential trades and is vendor research, not an HVAC-only government dataset.

Source:
https://www.servicetitan.com/press/residential-industry-report-2025

## EVIDENCE B - UNSOLD ESTIMATE FOLLOW-UP CAN BE ECONOMICALLY MATERIAL

The same 2025 ServiceTitan report said 47% of contractors with $10 million or more in annual revenue reported that following up on unsold estimates generated 11-15% of their income.

Approved wording:

> ServiceTitan's 2025 residential-contractor survey found that among respondents doing $10 million or more in annual revenue, 47% said unsold-estimate follow-up contributed 11-15% of income.

Do NOT say:

> Following up estimates will increase this contractor's revenue by 15%.

Limitation:

This is self-reported vendor survey data across residential trades and does not establish what any individual HVAC contractor will achieve.

Source:
https://www.servicetitan.com/press/residential-industry-report-2025

## EVIDENCE C - HVAC LABOR HAS REAL ECONOMIC VALUE

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics reports a median annual wage of $61,010 for heating, air conditioning, and refrigeration mechanics and installers in May 2025.

BLS projects employment in the occupation to grow 11% from 2025 to 2035, much faster than the 3% average for all occupations, with roughly 40,600 openings per year on average over the decade.

BLS also notes that HVAC technicians may work evenings, weekends, emergency on-call schedules, overtime, and irregular hours during peak heating and cooling seasons.

Approved sales implication:

> Skilled HVAC labor is economically valuable and demand is projected to grow. Systems that reduce avoidable administrative load around technicians and office staff may therefore create capacity value.

Do NOT turn median technician pay into the loaded labor cost of a specific contractor.

Source:
https://www.bls.gov/ooh/installation-maintenance-and-repair/heating-air-conditioning-and-refrigeration-mechanics-and-installers.htm

## EVIDENCE D - WORKFORCE PRESSURE IS A RECOGNIZED CONTRACTOR ISSUE

The Air Conditioning Contractors of America identifies the HVACR labor shortage as a major contractor concern and has made workforce development a policy priority.

Approved implication:

> Labor availability is a real industry concern, which makes employee-capacity and growth-without-proportional-overhead conversations relevant when the contractor confirms the problem locally.

Do NOT claim a specific national worker shortage number unless the source and methodology are verified.

Source:
https://www.acca.org/advocacy/government-relations/top-priorities

# 6. PRIMARY HVAC LEAKAGE POINTS

## 1. MISSED CALLS

Possible causes:

- all CSRs already on calls
- after-hours voicemail
- dispatch rush
- lunch / shift gaps
- seasonal volume surge
- insufficient overflow

## 2. SLOW LEAD RESPONSE

Possible causes:

- form sends only an email notification
- salesperson is driving / working
- lead is manually assigned
- no automatic acknowledgment
- no defined response SLA

## 3. WEAK FOLLOW-UP AFTER FIRST ATTEMPT

Possible causes:

- salesperson calls once
- no CRM task
- no sequence
- ownership unclear
- lead falls behind newer demand

## 4. UNSOLD REPLACEMENT ESTIMATES

Possible causes:

- comfort advisor moves to next lead
- no scheduled next step
- financing questions not followed up
- homeowner delay
- no long-term nurture
- no lost-reason data

## 5. AFTER-HOURS OPPORTUNITIES

Possible causes:

- voicemail
- answering service only takes a message
- on-call process inconsistent
- web forms wait until morning

## 6. SCHEDULING / RESCHEDULING LOAD

Possible causes:

- manual confirmations
- repeated phone tag
- no self-service options
- high seasonal reschedule volume

## 7. DISPATCH / CUSTOMER COMMUNICATION

Possible causes:

- repetitive ETA calls
- customers asking technician status
- manual notifications
- dispatchers answering avoidable questions

## 8. CRM / FIELD-SERVICE DATA GAPS

Possible causes:

- leads outside the platform
- calls not linked to customer records
- source attribution incomplete
- salespeople not updating status
- follow-up happening in personal phones

## 9. MARKETING ATTRIBUTION

Possible causes:

- Google reports a conversion but company cannot identify booked revenue
- calls lack source tracking
- replacement revenue is not connected back to campaign
- agency and sales data live separately

## 10. SERVICE-AGREEMENT / MAINTENANCE REACTIVATION

Possible causes:

- expired agreements
- overdue tune-ups
- past customers not segmented
- seasonal reminders inconsistent

## 11. REVIEW GENERATION

Possible causes:

- technician / CSR has to remember
- requests are inconsistent
- no post-job workflow

## 12. ADMINISTRATIVE CAPACITY

Possible causes:

- duplicate data entry
- CRM notes
- call summaries
- manual reports
- copying data between phone, field-service, CRM, accounting, and ad systems

# 21. HYPOTHETICAL MISSED-CALL EXAMPLE

This is an illustrative planning scenario only.

Suppose an HVAC contractor reports:

- 600 inbound calls per month
- 12% unanswered
- 60% of unanswered calls believed to be legitimate new-business opportunities
- 25% conservative scenario capture / close
- $750 average first-job revenue

Math:

600 x 12% = 72 unanswered calls

72 x 60% = 43.2 legitimate opportunities

43.2 x 25% = 10.8 scenario customers

10.8 x $750 = $8,100 illustrative monthly revenue scenario

This is NOT a forecast.

Every input must be labeled client data, client estimate, benchmark, or illustrative assumption.

The rep should then ask:

> Which of those inputs can we verify from your phone and job data?

# 40. HVAC-SPECIFIC OBJECTION - "CUSTOMERS WANT HUMANS"

Response:

> They may, especially when someone is uncomfortable in their home and needs help. We do not need to remove the human. We may be able to make sure the call is captured, the record is created, the right employee is alerted, and the routine follow-up happens consistently while a person handles the relationship and technical judgment.

# 41. HVAC-SPECIFIC OBJECTION - "AI CANNOT DIAGNOSE HVAC"

Response:

> I am not asking it to replace a qualified technician. Equipment diagnosis, repair decisions, code compliance, and safety-sensitive work should remain with qualified people. We are usually looking at the workflow around the technical work - intake, scheduling, follow-up, communication, CRM, reporting, and repetitive administration.

This is the approved YAD position.

# 42. SAFETY BOUNDARY

YAD salespeople must never represent AI as independently responsible for:

- HVAC diagnosis
- equipment safety decisions
- refrigerant handling decisions
- electrical safety decisions
- code compliance
- installation decisions
- repair authorization requiring qualified judgment
- emergency / hazard decisions outside an approved escalation workflow

Qualified technicians and responsible company personnel remain in control of technical and safety-sensitive work.

# 51. HVAC EVIDENCE CARDS - INITIAL APPROVED BANK

## CARD HVAC-001

Claim:
Phone remains a dominant customer-contact channel among residential service contractors.

Source:
ServiceTitan 2025 Residential Services Report.

Sample:
More than 1,000 U.S. residential service contractors across HVAC, electrical, plumbing, roofing, and other sectors.

Finding:
64% reported phone calls as the dominant form of communication / initial contact.

Tier:
C - credible vendor research, useful with caveat.

Approved wording:
> In ServiceTitan's 2025 survey of more than 1,000 residential service contractors, 64% said phone calls were their dominant customer-contact channel.

Forbidden overgeneralization:
> 64% of HVAC leads are phone calls.

Review date:
Revalidate annually or when newer ServiceTitan research is available.

## CARD HVAC-002

Claim:
Unsold-estimate follow-up can represent meaningful revenue among larger residential-service contractors.

Source:
ServiceTitan 2025 Residential Services Report.

Finding:
47% of $10M+ contractor respondents said unsold-estimate follow-up generated 11-15% of income.

Tier:
C - vendor research.

Approved wording:
Use exactly with sample/context.

Forbidden:
Promise that YAD follow-up will increase a contractor's revenue 11-15%.

## CARD HVAC-003

Claim:
HVAC technician labor is economically valuable and demand is projected to grow.

Median pay $61,010; projected employment growth 11%; approximately 40,600 openings annually on average over the decade.

Tier:
A - government.

Approved wording:
Use BLS numbers with dates and occupation name.

Forbidden:
Use median technician wage as a specific contractor's loaded labor cost.

Workforce availability is a recognized HVACR contractor concern.

Source:
Air Conditioning Contractors of America policy priorities / workforce materials.

Forbidden:
Invent a national shortage count without a sourced methodology.

# MODULE 10 CERTIFICATION STANDARD

A certified YAD HVAC salesperson should be able to:

- explain the HVAC business model in business language
- map the homeowner journey from demand through revenue and retention
- identify the most likely leakage points without assuming they exist
- use current research with proper caveats
- source active advertiser prospects through Google / Meta research
- conduct both a cold call and a walk-in conversation
- handle reception professionally
- discuss field-service / CRM systems without assuming replacement is necessary
- explain the economics of missed calls and unsold estimates conservatively
- use the correct ROI calculator
- explain employee capacity without replacement language
- respond to "we have ServiceTitan" and "we have an answering service"
- distinguish sales / administrative workflows from technical HVAC diagnosis
- preserve qualified human judgment in safety-sensitive work
- know when a contractor is already operating well and should be left alone

# CORE HVAC RULE

The HVAC salesperson is not selling "AI for HVAC."

They are learning where an HVAC company may be paying to create demand, failing to capture or follow up some of that demand, spending valuable employee time on repetitive work, or operating without clean visibility from lead to revenue.

Then they determine whether YAD has a defensible way to improve the system.
