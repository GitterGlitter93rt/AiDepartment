# MODULE 22 - ASSISTED LIVING / SENIOR CARE INDUSTRY PLAYBOOK

Status: Working Draft - Internal Sales Training
Vertical Priority: 13
Evidence Status: Workflow complete; market and occupancy statistics require source verification before final PDF.

## Purpose

Teach YAD salespeople how assisted-living and senior-care providers receive family inquiries, schedule tours, follow up future-timing leads, manage availability, communicate with families, process admissions documents, and improve staff capacity while keeping care, eligibility, clinical, legal, and placement decisions human.

Core question:

> Where are family inquiries, tours, follow-up, availability communication, administrative time, and referral-source visibility leaking between first contact and an appropriate move-in or other disposition?

---

# 1. WHY SENIOR CARE FITS YAD

Potential characteristics:

- emotional / urgent family inquiries
- phone-heavy lead intake
- website / referral leads
- delayed timing
- tours
- room / care-level availability
- multiple family decision-makers
- follow-up over weeks/months
- hospital / rehab / professional referral sources
- repetitive availability questions
- admission documentation
- resident/family communication
- privacy and care boundaries

---

# 2. BUSINESS MODEL

Potential providers:

- assisted living
- memory care
- independent living
- residential care homes
- larger senior living communities
- continuing care models

Revenue often depends on:

- occupancy
- room/unit type
- care level
- ancillary services
- length of stay

YAD reps should never assume rates or resident lifetime value.

# 5. AFTER-HOURS / URGENT INQUIRIES

Potential families call nights/weekends due to hospitalization, rehab discharge, or sudden change.

Potential YAD support:

- immediate acknowledgment
- basic non-clinical information capture
- tour scheduling where appropriate
- on-call / admissions routing
- CRM lead creation
- follow-up task

Do not let AI assess medical appropriateness or level of care.

---

# 18. OBJECTIONS

## "FAMILIES NEED A HUMAN"

> Absolutely. The human relationship is central. We're looking at the repetitive system around it - reminders, CRM tasks, scheduling, approved FAQs, document tracking, and reporting.

## "EVERY RESIDENT IS DIFFERENT"

> Care decisions are individual. We can still standardize the administrative workflow around inquiries, tours, documents, and follow-up.

## "WE CAN'T PUT HEALTH INFORMATION IN AI"

> Sensitive data should only be used in approved systems after technical/privacy review. Many opportunities can be designed without exposing clinical data.

# 19. ROI EXAMPLE - FUTURE LEAD FOLLOW-UP

HYPOTHETICAL:

- 120 inquiries/month
- 30% are future-timing rather than immediate
- 25% of those currently lose consistent follow-up
- 20% are ultimately a realistic fit when timing arrives
- $4,500 selected monthly revenue metric for an occupied unit

Future-timing = 36
Weak-follow-up exposure = 9
Realistic future-fit = 1.8

Do NOT multiply this directly into guaranteed resident revenue. Senior-living economics depend on availability, care fit, length of stay, timing, and family choice. Use this calculation only to show why the pipeline deserves measurement.

# 20. FIELD CHEAT SHEET

Best signals: active Google ads/directories, memory care, multiple communities, admissions team, high inquiry volume, visible room availability marketing.

Top hooks: future-timing follow-up; after-hours inquiry; tour no-show; referral attribution; admissions capacity.

Best first question:

> How do you stay connected with families who have a real need but aren't ready today?

ROI tools: Lead Response, No-Show, Employee Capacity.

Employee-safe: systems support admissions staff; care decisions and family relationships stay human.

---

# 21. CERTIFICATION

Rep must understand family inquiry lifecycle, future-timing leads, tours, CRM, privacy/care boundaries, referral attribution, conservative economics, and phone/walk-in behavior.

# MODULE 22 CORE RULE

Senior care is relationship-heavy and sensitive. YAD should make the admissions system more consistent and visible so staff can spend more time with families, while care, eligibility, health, and placement decisions remain with qualified humans.
