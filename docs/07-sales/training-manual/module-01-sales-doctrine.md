# Module 1 - The Your AI Department Sales Doctrine

Status: Approved Working Draft

## Purpose

Before a salesperson learns cold calls, scripts, objection handling, industry statistics, or ROI calculators, they must understand what Your AI Department actually sells.

Your AI Department does not sell artificial intelligence for the sake of artificial intelligence. We help businesses identify expensive problems, determine what those problems are costing them, and implement practical systems that improve business outcomes.

## Core doctrine

### Business problem first. Technology second.

Do not lead with AI agents, automation, replacement language, or technical terminology. Begin with the business workflow, the leak, the measurable impact, and the desired outcome.

The canonical Your AI Department Method is:

Assess -> Discover -> Prioritize -> Implement -> Adopt -> Optimize.

### Sell outcomes

Sell outcomes such as:
- more revenue
- more appointments
- faster response
- better follow-up
- higher employee capacity
- less repetitive work
- reduced overtime where appropriate
- better reporting and attribution
- better customer communication
- more scalable operations

### Permanent employee-positioning rule

Never position AI as a reason to fire or replace employees.

Preferred framing:
- increase employee capacity
- reduce repetitive administrative work
- help the existing team handle more volume
- reduce overtime where the workflow supports it
- allow employees to focus on higher-value work
- support growth without administrative overhead increasing at the same rate

Avoid:
- replace employees
- eliminate staff
- fire your receptionist
- cut half the office
- AI is cheaper than your employees

Attack the workflow, not the person.

## Five business leaks

1. Revenue leakage - missed calls, slow response, weak follow-up, unsold estimates, no-shows, old leads.
2. Marketing leakage - poor attribution, weak landing pages, poor lead quality, slow response after paid acquisition.
3. Labor leakage - repetitive administration, data entry, reporting, CRM updates, routine communication.
4. Capacity leakage - overloaded teams, overtime, backlogs, growth requiring proportional administrative headcount.
5. Information leakage - disconnected systems, weak reporting, inaccessible SOPs, poor visibility.

## Salesperson role

The salesperson is a business diagnostician, not an engineer.

The mental sequence is:

Where is the money? -> Where is it leaking? -> Why? -> How often? -> What is it worth? -> What could realistically improve? -> What should remain human? -> What is the simplest reliable solution? -> Is the business case strong enough to investigate further?

## Capability status

Salespeople must distinguish:
- Existing YAD Offering
- Implementation Capability
- Potential New Offering

Never invent a product, fixed package, integration, result, or commercial promise.

## Financial-claim rules

- Never present theoretical exposure as guaranteed recoverable revenue.
- Never invent case studies.
- Clearly label hypothetical examples and illustrative scenarios.
- Never guarantee revenue or ROI.
- Never promise a software integration without technical verification.
- Do not recommend AI simply because YAD can build it.
- Use the simplest reliable solution.

## Human + AI position

The default position is humans doing what humans do best, supported by systems handling repetitive, structured, and high-volume work where appropriate.

Examples:
- AI drafts; employee approves.
- AI captures; employee closes.
- AI summarizes; manager decides.
- AI organizes; professional reviews.
- AI handles routine workflows; humans handle exceptions.

## Sales commandments

1. Sell business outcomes, not AI terminology.
2. Diagnose before prescribing.
3. Never position the product as "fire your staff."
4. Increase employee capacity before discussing labor reduction.
5. Never invent statistics.
6. Never invent ROI.
7. Never invent case studies.
8. Never promise an integration before technical verification.
9. Never guarantee revenue.
10. Separate theoretical opportunity from realistically recoverable opportunity.
11. Do not recommend AI simply because we can build it.
12. Use the simplest reliable solution.
13. Keep human judgment where the workflow requires it.
14. Learn the economics of the prospect's business.
15. Ask questions until the business problem becomes measurable.
16. Protect credibility over winning a single objection.
17. Move the prospect toward the next logical commitment, not necessarily the largest sale.
18. Measure results after implementation.
19. Use actual client results to improve future recommendations.
20. Make the business better, not merely more automated.

## Pass standard

A salesperson passes Module 1 when they can explain YAD without technical jargon and can clearly articulate:

"We find where the business is losing money, opportunities, time, capacity, or visibility - and determine whether better systems can fix it."
