# MODULE 13 - COLLISION REPAIR INDUSTRY PLAYBOOK

Status: Working Draft - Internal Sales Training
Vertical Priority: 4

## Purpose

This playbook teaches YAD salespeople how collision repair businesses acquire customers, answer estimate inquiries, schedule estimates, interact with insurers, communicate repair status, manage production/admin workflows, follow up unsold estimates, and measure marketing performance.

The salesperson diagnoses business workflow, not repair procedures.

Core question:

> Where are estimate opportunities, front-office capacity, customer communication, administrative work, and marketing attribution leaking between the first inquiry and the completed paid repair?

---

# 1. WHY COLLISION REPAIR IS A STRONG YAD VERTICAL

Collision repair businesses may combine:

- urgent / emotional customer inquiries
- phone-heavy intake
- estimate scheduling
- insurer-related administration
- high document volume
- parts / production communication
- customer-status calls
- high-value repair orders
- unsold estimates
- Google search intent
- local reviews
- shop-management software
- multi-location reporting
- hail-event demand surges
- increasingly complex vehicle technology

This creates potential opportunities across customer acquisition, workflow, employee capacity, communication, CRM, reporting, and integrations.

# 13. CRM VS SHOP-MANAGEMENT SOFTWARE

A collision shop may have estimating / management software but still lack a clean marketing/sales CRM layer.

Reps must understand:

SHOP-MANAGEMENT SYSTEM
may manage repair production.

CRM / GROWTH SYSTEM
may manage pre-repair leads, source, follow-up, attribution, reactivation, and marketing communication.

Sometimes one platform can handle both pieces; sometimes integration is required.

Do not assume a new CRM is necessary.

Ask:

- Where are pre-estimate leads stored?
- Can unsold estimates be segmented?
- Can the source be tied to repair revenue?
- Are calls tracked?
- Can follow-up tasks be generated?
- Can management see stale opportunities?

# 20. ROI EXAMPLE - UNSOLD ESTIMATES

HYPOTHETICAL ONLY.

Assume:

- 180 estimates/month
- 65% become repair orders
- 63 remain unsold
- 40% of unsold estimates are realistically still addressable
- 6% incremental close scenario
- $5,200 average repair revenue

Addressable = 63 x 40% = 25.2
Scenario additional repairs = 25.2 x 6% = 1.512
Illustrative monthly revenue = 1.512 x $5,200 = $7,862.40

This is not guaranteed. Use the shop's actual estimate counts, conversion, loss reasons, and repair revenue.

Do not substitute CCC's national repair-cost statistic for client value.

---

# 21. ROI EXAMPLE - CUSTOMER UPDATE CAPACITY

HYPOTHETICAL.

Assume:

- 3 CSRs
- 4 hours/week each on repetitive status communication / manual notes
- 48 weeks
- 45% addressable
- $31 loaded hourly cost

Annual repetitive hours = 3 x 4 x 48 = 576
Addressable = 576 x 45% = 259.2
Potential capacity value = 259.2 x $31 = $8,035.20

This represents potential capacity, not guaranteed payroll savings.

# 28. COLLISION STORIES

## Front Desk Bottleneck

HYPOTHETICAL: CSR handles pickup + insurer + rental issue; estimate caller gets voicemail and calls another shop.

Question:

> How often does the system—not the employee—run out of simultaneous capacity?

## Status Call Loop

HYPOTHETICAL: customer calls; CSR interrupts estimator/production to find status; gives update; repeats with dozens of customers.

Question:

> Which of those updates could be generated from reliable status data before the customer has to call?

## Unsold Estimate

HYPOTHETICAL: customer receives estimate, needs to decide, no defined task exists, lead disappears.

Question:

> Can you see every estimate in that exact status today?

# 29. COLLISION OBJECTIONS

## "OUR MANAGEMENT SOFTWARE ALREADY DOES THAT"

> Good. Then the question is whether the feature is configured, consistently used, and connected to the rest of your lead / communication workflow. If it already solves the problem well, we leave it alone.

## "WE GET WORK FROM INSURANCE; WE DON'T NEED MARKETING"

> That may be true for part of the mix. How do you measure your direct, dealer, referral, prior-customer, and other lead channels today?

## "CUSTOMERS WANT HUMAN UPDATES"

> They may. We can preserve human conversations while automating reminders, routine notifications, summaries, and internal data movement around them.

## "WE DON'T WANT AI TOUCHING REPAIR DECISIONS"

> Neither do we unless a narrowly defined workflow is appropriate. Qualified repair decisions and safety-critical work stay with qualified people.

## "WE HAVE TOO MANY SYSTEMS ALREADY"

> Then adding another system may be the wrong answer. We first look at whether the existing systems can be configured or connected better.

# 30. WHEN NOT TO SELL

Do not force a project if:

- shop has minimal direct lead opportunity
- estimate follow-up is already excellent
- customer-status data is unreliable and no integration path exists
- economics do not justify implementation
- company demands repair/safety decisions be automated irresponsibly
- integration is not feasible
- owner wants guarantees
- primary objective is staff elimination

## Top Five Hooks

3. Customer status calls

## Best First Question

> Roughly how many estimate inquiries do you see, what percentage never schedule, and what is an average completed repair worth from your own data?

# MODULE 13 CORE RULE

Collision shops already operate in a complex technical environment. YAD should not add technology for technology's sake. We find where customer opportunity, information, or employee capacity breaks down and use the simplest reliable system to improve it while qualified repair professionals remain responsible for the repair itself.
