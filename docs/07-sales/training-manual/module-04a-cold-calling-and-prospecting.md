# MODULE 4A - COLD CALLING AND PROSPECTING

Status: Working Draft - Internal Sales Training

## Purpose

Cold calling for Your AI Department is not a contest to see how quickly a salesperson can explain AI.

The objective is to create enough relevance and curiosity to earn a real business conversation.

The rep is trying to discover whether a prospect has meaningful problems involving:

- missed opportunities
- slow lead response
- weak follow-up
- poor attribution
- repetitive administration
- employee capacity constraints
- disconnected systems
- reporting / visibility gaps
- customer-service bottlenecks
- growth that may require unnecessary administrative overhead

The call should connect naturally into the YAD discovery and financial-diagnosis framework.

---

# 1. THE PURPOSE OF A COLD CALL

A cold call normally has one of four successful outcomes:

1. Speak with the right decision-maker and uncover a meaningful problem.
2. Earn a scheduled discovery / strategy conversation.
3. Identify the correct decision-maker and next contact step.
4. Disqualify the prospect quickly and professionally.

The purpose is usually NOT to sell a full implementation on the first call.

A rep should not unload every YAD service, explain technical architecture, or attempt to price an undefined solution.

---

# 2. THE CORE PROSPECTING DOCTRINE

## Do not lead with AI.

Weak:

> We are an AI automation company and build AI agents.

Stronger:

> We help businesses find where leads, follow-up, employee time, or reporting are falling through the cracks and determine whether better systems can improve it.

Business owners buy outcomes.

Technology comes later.

---

# 3. THE FIRST TEN SECONDS

The first ten seconds should establish:

- who you are
- that you are not pretending this is a warm call
- why the conversation may be relevant
- a simple question

Recommended default opener:

> Hey [Name], this is [Rep] with Your AI Department. This is a cold call, so I will be brief. I had a question about how your company handles [specific business process].

Examples:

> ...what happens when a new lead comes in and nobody gets to it immediately.

> ...what happens to estimates that do not close on the first conversation.

> ...how much of your office team's week is still spent manually moving information between systems.

> ...how you track which marketing actually turns into revenue.

The honesty is the pattern interrupt.

Do not use fake familiarity.

Do not pretend someone referred you when they did not.

Do not say you are returning a call when you are not.

---

# 4. ALTERNATIVE OPENERS

## Permission opener

> Hey [Name], [Rep] with Your AI Department. I know I am calling you out of nowhere. Can I give you the 20-second reason I called, and you can tell me if it is irrelevant?

## Business-process opener

> Hey [Name], quick question. When a new lead calls after hours or your office is tied up, what happens today?

## Follow-up opener

> Hey [Name], I was curious about something in your sales process. What happens to a quote or estimate that does not close the first time?

## Capacity opener

> Hey [Name], quick question: if your company grew 50% next year, where would you have to add office staff first?

## Attribution opener

> Hey [Name], do you know all the way down to revenue which lead sources are actually producing customers for you?

The rep should select an opener based on the prospect, not randomly rotate scripts.

---

# 5. TWO-MINUTE PRE-CALL RESEARCH

Before calling, the rep should know enough to avoid sounding generic.

Review quickly:

- company website
- primary service lines
- service area / locations
- business size clues
- phone / booking experience
- visible lead forms
- reviews and common customer complaints
- whether the company appears to advertise heavily
- whether multiple locations or departments exist
- obvious CRM / scheduling / intake clues if publicly visible

The goal is not a 30-minute investigation.

The goal is one relevant hypothesis.

Example:

> Multi-location HVAC company + heavy paid search + after-hours emergency service = potential missed-call, attribution, scheduling, and lead-response conversation.

---

# 6. THE PROBLEM HYPOTHESIS

Every outbound attempt should have a reason.

Use this formula:

BUSINESS MODEL
+
VISIBLE WORKFLOW
+
LIKELY LEAK
=
PROSPECTING HYPOTHESIS

Example:

Roofing company
+
high-ticket estimates
+
long manual follow-up
=
unsold-estimate opportunity.

Law firm
+
intake-heavy business
+
after-hours inquiries
=
intake / response opportunity.

Collision shop
+
phone-heavy customer journey
+
estimates and insurer administration
=
missed-call + follow-up + admin-capacity opportunity.

The hypothesis is a reason to ask a question.

It is NOT permission to claim the problem definitely exists.

---

# 7. THE OPENING CONVERSATION FRAMEWORK

Use:

## OPEN

Establish who you are and why you called.

## QUESTION

Ask one operational question.

## LISTEN

Do not interrupt the answer with a pitch.

## PROBE

Understand frequency, process, and impact.

## QUANTIFY

If appropriate, put numbers around the problem.

## POSITION

Explain how YAD approaches this category.

## NEXT STEP

Earn a deeper discovery / strategy conversation.

---

# 8. THE FIRST DIAGNOSTIC QUESTIONS

High-value early questions include:

- What happens when a new lead does not answer the first callback?
- How quickly are new inbound leads normally contacted?
- What happens to calls after hours?
- Do you know what percentage of inbound calls are missed?
- What happens to estimates or proposals that do not close immediately?
- Are leads consistently tracked in a CRM?
- Can you tie your marketing spend to actual collected revenue?
- What administrative work eats the most employee time every week?
- Where are employees copying information manually between systems?
- If volume increased 50%, where would the operation break first?
- What report does management wish it could see instantly but cannot?

Do not ask all of these in sequence.

The first call is a conversation, not an interrogation.

---

# 9. THE 30-SECOND YAD EXPLANATION

If the prospect asks:

> So what exactly do you guys do?

Recommended answer:

> We look at how the business generates leads, handles customers, follows up, moves information, and uses employee time. We identify where money, opportunities, capacity, or visibility are leaking, then determine whether AI, automation, better software, integrations, training, or marketing systems can realistically improve it. We are not trying to replace your staff. The goal is better systems around the people you already have.

Short version:

> We find expensive workflow problems and help businesses fix them with the right combination of AI, automation, software, and process improvement.

---

# 10. DO NOT FEATURE-DUMP

If the owner describes missed calls, do not respond with:

> We have AI voice agents, LLMs, CRM integrations, SMS sequences, APIs, webhooks...

Instead:

> That sounds worth measuring. Roughly how many inbound calls are you handling in a month?

Problem first.

Economics second.

Solution third.

---

# 11. THE MONEY-LEAK TRANSITION

When a real problem appears, transition into financial diagnosis.

Example:

Owner:

> Yeah, we probably miss some calls when everyone is busy.

Rep:

> Do you have any idea how many calls a month that might be?

Owner:

> Maybe 40 or 50.

Rep:

> And of those, what percentage do you think are actually legitimate new-business opportunities rather than vendors, existing customers, spam, things like that?

Owner:

> Half maybe.

Rep:

> What is a new customer normally worth on the first job?

Now the conversation has moved from AI to economics.

Do not claim every missed call would become a customer.

---

# 12. GATEKEEPER HANDLING

Gatekeepers are not enemies.

They are doing their job.

Never become disrespectful, deceptive, or pushy.

## Default gatekeeper line

> Totally fair. I am not trying to pitch software to you at the front desk. I am trying to figure out who owns [lead handling / operations / marketing / customer intake / sales process]. Who would normally be the right person for that?

## If they ask what it is about

> We work with businesses on things like missed opportunities, follow-up, administrative workload, and how their systems connect. I had one specific question about how you handle [process].

## If they refuse access

> No problem. What is the best way to get a short note to them so they can decide if it is relevant?

Do not lie to bypass a gatekeeper.

---

# 13. WHEN THE OWNER SAYS THEY ARE BUSY

Do not fight them.

> Completely understand. Give me ten seconds and you can decide whether I should disappear.

Then use ONE question.

Example:

> When a new lead does not get answered immediately, do you have a system that keeps working it until somebody connects?

If yes and strong:

> Great. Sounds like that part may already be handled. I will not waste your time.

If no:

> That is exactly why I called. I would rather schedule a proper conversation than try to diagnose it while you are busy.

---

# 14. SEND ME AN EMAIL

Do not respond with a desperate pitch.

Use the request to qualify what the email should contain.

> Absolutely. So I do not send you generic AI garbage, which is more relevant to you: lead handling, employee/admin workload, marketing performance, or something else?

Then:

> Perfect. I will keep it short and make it about that.

The email should reference the exact problem discussed.

Do not send a giant capability brochure unless specifically requested.

---

# 15. WE ALREADY USE CHATGPT

> That is good. We are not selling access to ChatGPT. The question is whether your actual business workflows - lead handling, CRM, follow-up, reporting, scheduling, internal knowledge, marketing attribution - are designed around the right tools and processes.

Follow-up:

> Where has your team actually integrated AI into a repeatable workflow today?

---

# 16. WE ALREADY HAVE A RECEPTIONIST

> Good. We are not looking to replace her. The question would be whether there are overflow, after-hours, repetitive intake, scheduling, or follow-up tasks that could make the front office more capable.

Follow-up:

> Where does the receptionist get overloaded today?

---

# 17. WE ALREADY HAVE A CRM

> Great. A CRM is usually part of the answer, not the entire answer. How consistently is it actually used, and what happens automatically after a lead enters it?

---

# 18. WE HAVE AN IT COMPANY

> That can be a good thing. We are not automatically replacing IT either. IT usually owns infrastructure, security, support, and technology operations. We focus on identifying business workflows and measurable opportunities, then implementation may involve the existing IT team where appropriate.

---

# 19. WE HAVE A MARKETING AGENCY

> Good. We are not assuming the agency is the problem. We would want to understand the entire chain from ad spend to lead to response to sales to revenue. Sometimes the opportunity is marketing. Sometimes it is what happens after the lead arrives.

---

# 20. VOICEMAIL

Keep it short.

> Hey [Name], [Rep] with Your AI Department. I had a quick question about how your company handles [specific workflow]. Nothing urgent. I will try you again, or you can reach me at [number]. Again, [Rep] with Your AI Department.

Do not give a full pitch in voicemail.

---

# 21. FOLLOW-UP CALLS

A second call should not sound like:

> Just checking in.

Bring a reason.

Examples:

> I wanted to follow up on the estimate-follow-up question I left you.

> I was looking at your after-hours service model and had one question about what happens to calls when the office closes.

> I sent the short note on employee capacity. I wanted to see whether that is actually relevant before I keep bothering you.

Respect an explicit request to stop contact.

---

# 22. BOOKING THE NEXT STEP

Do not overcomplicate the close.

> Based on what you just told me, I think this is worth a proper look. Rather than guess on the phone, the next step would be to map the workflow, get the real numbers, and see whether there is actually a business case. Would you be open to that?

If yes:

> Great. Let us get the right conversation on the calendar.

Use the current approved YAD assessment / strategy-call process. Do not hard-code a scheduling platform or assessment price in rep scripts until the repository's current commercial workflow is reconciled.

---

# 23. DISQUALIFICATION

A good salesperson protects time.

Potential reasons to deprioritize:

- no meaningful pain
- extremely low volume
- problem value too small
- no decision access
- no urgency
- no realistic budget for any improvement
- prospect wants guaranteed revenue
- prospect expects unethical / unsafe automation
- existing workflow is already strong
- YAD capability is not a fit

Professional exit:

> It sounds like this may not be a priority right now. I would rather say that than force a project where the business case is weak.

---

# 24. CRM OUTCOMES

Every outbound attempt should end with a recorded outcome.

Minimum useful outcomes:

- no answer
- voicemail
- gatekeeper - decision-maker identified
- wrong contact
- spoke - no pain
- spoke - possible opportunity
- follow-up requested
- email requested
- discovery / strategy conversation scheduled
- disqualified
- do not contact

Useful notes:

- problem discussed
- prospect's exact wording
- numbers mentioned
- decision-maker
- timing / urgency
- current systems
- next action

Do not write insulting notes about prospects or employees.

---

# 25. CALL SCORECARD

A manager should be able to grade a call on:

1. Relevant preparation
2. Honest opening
3. Clear reason for calling
4. Quality of first question
5. Listening
6. Follow-up questions
7. Business language instead of AI jargon
8. Financial diagnosis when appropriate
9. Employee-safe positioning
10. No invented claims
11. Clear next step
12. Accurate CRM documentation

---

# 26. ROLEPLAY DRILLS

Every salesperson should practice:

- friendly owner
- skeptical owner
- owner in a hurry
- receptionist / gatekeeper
- "send me an email"
- "we already use ChatGPT"
- "we have a receptionist"
- "we already have a CRM"
- "we have an IT company"
- "we have a marketing agency"
- no obvious pain
- strong pain but no reliable numbers
- strong opportunity with a clear next step

---

# 27. MODULE 4A PASS STANDARD

A salesperson passes when they can:

- open a cold call without sounding deceptive or generic
- explain YAD without technical jargon
- ask one relevant process question quickly
- transition from pain to measurable impact
- avoid turning theoretical exposure into promised ROI
- avoid attacking employees
- handle a gatekeeper professionally
- respond to common brush-offs without becoming pushy
- book an appropriate next step
- disqualify a weak opportunity
- document the call accurately

The rep should sound like a business diagnostician who happens to understand AI - not an AI salesperson hunting for somewhere to install software.
