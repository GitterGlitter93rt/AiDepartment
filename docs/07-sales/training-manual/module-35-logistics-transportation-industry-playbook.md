# MODULE 35 - LOGISTICS / TRANSPORTATION INDUSTRY PLAYBOOK

Status: Working Draft - Internal Sales Training
Evidence Status: Workflow complete; sector-specific benchmarks require verified sourcing before final PDF.

## Purpose

Teach YAD salespeople how logistics, trucking, brokerage, and transportation companies acquire accounts, quote freight, coordinate loads, communicate status, process documents, invoice customers, and report performance while dispatch, safety, regulatory, and operational decisions remain under qualified human control.

Core question:

> Where are sales opportunities, dispatch coordination, shipment information, document processing, customer communication, and reporting consuming unnecessary employee capacity or losing visibility?

# 5. CUSTOMER STATUS COMMUNICATION

Repetitive questions:

- pickup confirmed?
- ETA?
- delivered?
- POD available?
- delay?

Potential support: status-trigger notifications from reliable TMS/telematics data, ticket routing, summaries.

# 9. ACCOUNTS RECEIVABLE

Potential workflow: invoice -> due -> reminder -> escalation -> human collections.

# 10. SAFETY / REGULATORY BOUNDARY

Automation does not make unsafe dispatch, hours-of-service, hazmat, vehicle-safety, driver-fitness, or regulatory decisions without qualified human oversight and compliant systems.

# 15. ROI EXAMPLE - STATUS CAPACITY

HYPOTHETICAL:

- 6 operations employees
- 5 hours/week each on repetitive status/admin communication
- 48 weeks
- 35% addressable
- $38 loaded hourly cost

Annual repetitive = 1,440 hours
Addressable = 504
Potential annual capacity = $19,152

Not payroll savings.

---

# 16. FIELD CHEAT SHEET

Signals: high load volume, multiple dispatchers, 24/7, TMS, broker/3PL sales team, visible hiring.

Top hooks: dispatch communication; POD/document processing; CRM/account reactivation; integrations; reporting.

Employee-safe: automate repetitive coordination; dispatch/safety/compliance decisions stay human.

# MODULE 35 CORE RULE

Logistics companies live in information movement. YAD should reduce repetitive communication and data movement while dispatchers, drivers, and qualified operators retain responsibility for real-world transportation decisions.
