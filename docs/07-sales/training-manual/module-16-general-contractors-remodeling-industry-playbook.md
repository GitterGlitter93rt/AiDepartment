# MODULE 16 - GENERAL CONTRACTORS / REMODELING INDUSTRY PLAYBOOK

Status: Working Draft - Internal Sales Training
Vertical Priority: 7
Evidence Status: Core workflow grounded in existing YAD construction playbook; selected market-size evidence requires final publication verification.

## Purpose

Teach YAD salespeople how general contractors and remodeling companies generate leads, qualify projects, schedule estimates, prepare proposals, manage follow-up, coordinate customers/subcontractors/vendors, handle project documents, and scale administrative capacity.

The salesperson diagnoses business workflow, not construction engineering or code compliance.

Core question:

> Where are qualified project leads, estimate opportunities, project information, employee capacity, and management visibility leaking between the first inquiry and a completed profitable project?

---

# 1. WHY THIS VERTICAL FITS YAD

Common characteristics:

- high-value projects
- long sales cycles
- estimates / proposals
- multiple customer touches
- paid search / SEO / referrals
- project-management software
- subcontractor coordination
- change orders
- document volume
- scheduling complexity
- repetitive status communication
- accounting / CRM / project software fragmentation
- owner dependency

YAD can create value even when the contractor does not need a sophisticated AI agent. CRM, integration, reporting, follow-up, templates, and process redesign may be the better answer.

# 4. HIGH-VALUE LEAD QUALIFICATION

A GC may waste substantial estimating time on poor-fit leads.

Questions:

- What projects are ideal?
- What geography?
- Minimum project size?
- Timeline?
- Ownership / decision-maker status?
- Financing / budget readiness?
- Design status?

AI or automation can collect structured information, but final project acceptance remains human.

# 18. STORIES

## The Forgotten Proposal

HYPOTHETICAL: estimator spends hours preparing a high-value proposal; customer says think about it; next week fills with new site visits; no next task exists.

Ask:

> How does your company keep that proposal alive without relying on memory?

## The Double Entry

HYPOTHETICAL: customer data moves manually CRM -> estimating -> project-management -> accounting.

Ask:

> Where does the same project information get typed again today?

---

# 19. OBJECTIONS

## "OUR PROJECTS ARE TOO CUSTOM TO AUTOMATE"

> The project decisions may be highly custom. We're usually looking at the repeatable workflow around those decisions - lead capture, scheduling, document routing, reminders, reporting, and communication.

## "WE ALREADY HAVE BUILDER SOFTWARE"

> Good. What does it still require employees to do manually, and does it connect cleanly to your sales and accounting workflow?

## "WE WIN MOST WORK THROUGH REFERRALS"

> Great. How do you track, follow up, and measure those referral opportunities today?

# 20. ROI EXAMPLE - PROPOSAL FOLLOW-UP

HYPOTHETICAL:

- 30 proposals/month
- 12 remain unsold after initial decision window
- 50% still realistic opportunities
- 8% incremental close scenario
- $35,000 average sold project revenue

Addressable = 12 x 50% = 6
Scenario wins = 6 x 8% = 0.48
Illustrative monthly revenue scenario = $16,800

Do not promise fractional jobs or automatic revenue. The model shows economic leverage; use client data and realistic time horizons.

---

# 21. FIELD CHEAT SHEET

Best signals: active Google Ads, high-value remodel services, multiple estimators, strong reviews, multiple crews, project-management software, hiring.

Top hooks: proposal follow-up; lead qualification; duplicate admin; customer communication; attribution.

Best first question:

> What happens to a high-value proposal that doesn't close the first time?

ROI tool: Unsold Estimate ROI or Employee Capacity ROI.

Employee-safe position:

> We want estimators and project managers spending more time on customers and decisions, with routine tracking and administration handled more consistently.

---

# 22. SAFETY / PROFESSIONAL BOUNDARIES

AI does not independently decide:

- structural design
- engineering
- code compliance
- site safety
- contract/legal terms
- pricing approval
- major financial commitments

Qualified humans remain responsible.

---

# 23. CERTIFICATION

Rep must map the GC customer journey, diagnose proposal follow-up, explain CRM/project integration, quantify capacity conservatively, deliver phone/walk-in scripts, preserve construction safety/professional boundaries, and know when the economics do not justify a project.

# MODULE 16 CORE RULE

Construction value is often trapped in handoffs: lead to estimator, estimator to proposal, proposal to contract, and project data from one system to another. YAD improves those handoffs around qualified construction professionals rather than pretending the project itself is a generic automation.
