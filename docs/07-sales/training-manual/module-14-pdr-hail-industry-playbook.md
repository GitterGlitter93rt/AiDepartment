# MODULE 14 - PDR / HAIL INDUSTRY PLAYBOOK

Status: Working Draft - Internal Sales Training
Vertical Priority: 5

## Purpose

This playbook teaches YAD salespeople how paintless dent repair and automotive hail businesses generate and capture demand before, during, and after hail events; how they manage temporary lead surges, appointments, insurance/customer administration, field sales, follow-up, production communication, reviews, and multi-market operations.

The YAD rep diagnoses business workflow, not vehicle damage or insurance coverage.

Core question:

> When hail creates a temporary market full of vehicle owners who need help, how reliably does the business turn that surge into trackable appointments, repair opportunities, completed vehicles, customer communication, and measurable revenue?

---

# 1. WHY PDR / HAIL IS ITS OWN YAD VERTICAL

Hail repair differs from ordinary collision because demand can be:

- event-driven
- geographically concentrated
- temporary
- extremely high-volume
- mobile / multi-market
- dependent on rapid setup
- dependent on field sales and local marketing
- heavily appointment and communication driven
- insurance-connected
- sensitive to weather timing and customer urgency

A company may need to stand up a market quickly, process large lead volume, move between cities, and keep operational data consistent across temporary or permanent locations.

# 3. PUBLIC MARKET CONTEXT - HAIL EVENTS

Insurance Information Institute's hail statistics, citing NOAA/NWS Storm Prediction Center data, reported 5,432 U.S. hail events of one inch or larger in 2025.

Triple-I also reported that severe convective storms - including tornadoes, hail, straight-line winds, and severe thunderstorms - generated $51 billion in U.S. insured losses in 2025.

Approved wording:

> Hail is a recurring U.S. severe-weather exposure, and individual events can create sudden localized demand for automotive repair.

Do NOT say:

> There is $51 billion of PDR work available.

The $51 billion figure spans multiple severe-convective-storm perils and insured property categories, not automotive PDR alone.

# 21. ROI EXAMPLE - HAIL LEAD SURGE

HYPOTHETICAL ONLY.

Assume:

- 800 new leads during an event window
- 12% not contacted within the company's desired response window
- 55% of those are legitimate vehicle opportunities
- 20% scenario recovery / conversion
- $3,500 average completed repair revenue

Delayed/unhandled = 800 x 12% = 96
Legitimate = 96 x 55% = 52.8
Scenario repairs = 52.8 x 20% = 10.56
Illustrative revenue scenario = 10.56 x $3,500 = $36,960 across the event window

This is an illustrative model only. Replace with actual lead logs, status data, repair conversion, and customer revenue.

Do not imply all slow leads were lost or that all could be recovered.

HYPOTHETICAL: hail hits Saturday; 300 calls/forms/social leads arrive; staff creates multiple spreadsheets; Monday management cannot identify duplicates or untouched leads.

Question:

> What part of that happens in your operation today?

## The Field Rep's Phone

HYPOTHETICAL: salesperson has 60 prospects saved in their phone, some text threads, some notes. When the rep leaves town, management loses pipeline visibility.

Question:

> Does the company own the lead record, or does the rep's phone own it?

## The No-Show Appointment

HYPOTHETICAL: valuable inspection slot sits unused because reminder/reschedule process was inconsistent.

Question:

> What happens to that customer automatically after the missed appointment?

# 29. PDR / HAIL OBJECTIONS

## "HAIL IS TOO FAST FOR A CRM"

> That speed is exactly why a lightweight, well-designed pipeline can matter. The CRM should reduce work, not create data-entry bureaucracy. We'd design around what reps can actually use in the field.

## "OUR GUYS KEEP EVERYTHING IN THEIR PHONES"

> That can work at very small scale. The business question is whether management can see and protect the pipeline when multiple reps and markets are involved.

## "WE JUST NEED MORE LEADS"

> Maybe. Before buying more, I'd want to know what percentage of current leads are contacted, scheduled, shown, and converted. More demand into a leaking process can increase waste.

## "EVERY STORM IS DIFFERENT"

> The market is different, but parts of the operating system can still be standardized: lead capture, source tracking, assignment, appointment, follow-up, status, and reporting.

## "WE DON'T WANT AI TALKING ABOUT INSURANCE"

> Then we design it not to. Claim-specific, coverage, legal, and sensitive questions can route to qualified humans. AI or automation can still handle scheduling, intake, reminders, and internal workflows.

# 30. WHEN NOT TO SELL

Do not force a project when:

- operator has tiny lead volume
- current field/CRM process is already strong
- campaign economics are weak
- no reliable data or measurement can be established
- business wants misleading insurance representations
- company refuses communication compliance
- technical integrations are not feasible
- owner demands guarantees
- automation would compromise repair / claim judgment

# MODULE 14 CORE RULE

Hail creates temporary urgency. The company that can rapidly capture, organize, schedule, follow up, communicate, and measure the opportunity has an operational advantage. YAD's job is to build that repeatable business system around the people doing the selling and repairing - not to make claim, insurance, or repair decisions that belong to qualified humans.
