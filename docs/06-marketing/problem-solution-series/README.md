# YAD Business Problem Series

Status: Active creative-development system
Updated: 2026-09-04

## Purpose

This series turns the Your AI Department sales doctrine into short-form video creative.

The governing rule is **business problem first, technology second**. Each ad should make a business owner recognize a real workflow before introducing AI, automation, CRM, integrations, or marketing systems.

The series is built around the same visualization structure used in the sales manual:

**Current state -> Breakpoint -> Business consequence -> Better workflow -> Outcome category**

The story illustrates the workflow; it is not proof of a result. Avoid invented case studies, unsupported statistics, guarantees, and employee-replacement framing.

## Permanent creative doctrine

1. Attack the workflow, not the employee.
2. Show competent people encountering capacity, consistency, information, or system limits.
3. Do not imply AI replaces good employees.
4. AI may add capacity, consistency, coverage, routing, organization, or visibility.
5. Human work remains where judgment, relationship, negotiation, exceptions, physical coordination, or professional review are needed.
6. Do not call lead generation, sales, follow-up, or customer service "low-value work." The point is that valuable work deserves enough capacity.
7. The visual action must prove the business logic. A narrator saying something happened is not enough if the scene contradicts it.
8. Exact UI, workflow stages, brand text, domains, and claims should be deterministic post-production whenever possible.
9. Each substantive revision gets its own local and Dropbox folder.
10. Every final asset should be remotely verified after upload.

## Priority order after Creative #2

### Priority 1 - Creative #3: After-Hours Opportunity

Why next: immediately understandable, visually different from the first two, high relevance across HVAC, plumbing, legal, collision, roofing, dental, med spa, real estate, and other appointment-driven businesses.

Core line:

> Your office may close. New customers do not always wait until morning.

### Priority 2 - Creative #4: Double Entry

Why next: proves YAD is not just a phone-agent company. It introduces integration and workflow automation using a problem every office employee understands.

Core line:

> If your team is typing the same information into three systems, that may be a systems problem - not a staffing problem.

### Priority 3 - Creative #5: Attribution Blind Spot

Why next: positions YAD as growth infrastructure rather than generic AI. It connects ads, calls/forms, CRM, appointments, and collected revenue.

Core line:

> A lead is not the finish line. Can you trace the ad to the sale?

### Priority 4 - Creative #6: Appointment No-Show / Recovery

Why next: broad applicability and easy visual storytelling. Adds reminder, confirmation, and rescheduling use cases without implying employee replacement.

### Priority 5 - Creative #7: Dormant Lead Reactivation

Why next: expands the sales-capacity narrative from new leads to existing databases while avoiding the false claim that every old record is recoverable revenue.

### Priority 6 - Creative #8: Manager Knowledge Bottleneck

Why next: introduces internal AI and knowledge systems. Shows YAD can improve employee capacity without touching phones or paid marketing.

## Creative production rules learned from the first two ads

- Use off-camera narration when possible so narration can be replaced without rerolling good visuals.
- If the narrator is off camera, do not spend for a new video solely to fix pronunciation.
- Prefer full-track voice replacement over isolated word patches.
- Avoid words that repeatedly fail TTS/Seedance pronunciation when a natural synonym exists.
- Telephone scenes require causal visual proof: initiation, ringback, no answer, logging, etc.
- Background office audio should support the story and remain audible on a phone speaker.
- Do not accept robotic sighing, repeated nodding, vacant pauses, or generic "busy" acting. Give actors purposeful tasks.
- Avoid generated floating AR/UI artifacts; exact interfaces belong in deterministic post.
- Progressive cards must use active-content optical centering and causal reveal order.
- Keep narration conversational. Graphics carry lists and process detail.

- `QC/`
- `META/`

A new substantive revision must never be silently placed inside a prior revision's folder.
