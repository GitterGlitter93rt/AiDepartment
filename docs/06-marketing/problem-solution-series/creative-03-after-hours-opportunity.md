# Creative 03 - After-Hours Opportunity

Status: Script ready
Series: YAD Business Problem Series

## Business problem

A new customer reaches out after normal office hours. The advertising, referral, search result, or website did its job, but the office is closed. The opportunity either waits for the morning or the customer keeps looking.

This is not an employee failure. It is a coverage gap.

## Core message

**Your office may close. New customers do not always wait until morning.**

YAD can add after-hours response capacity so appropriate inbound opportunities can be answered, captured, routed, and booked without asking staff to remain available around the clock.

## Positioning guardrails

- Do not imply every after-hours caller becomes a customer.
- Do not guarantee bookings or revenue.
- Do not imply the office should eliminate staff.
- Do not call after-hours human coverage a waste.
- Keep emergency/professional escalation rules business-defined.
- For legal, medical, or other regulated verticals, do not present the agent as making professional judgments.

## Preferred 30-second narration

> Your office may close at five, but new customers do not always call during business hours. When nobody can answer, that opportunity either waits until morning or starts looking somewhere else. Your AI Department can add an after-hours AI agent that answers the call, captures what the customer needs, follows your business rules, and books or routes the next step. Your team comes back to organized opportunities instead of a voicemail pile. Same team. More coverage. Your AI Department.

## Visual story

### 0-5 sec - Office closes

- Employee turns off monitor / gathers belongings / exits normally.
- Front office lights dim.
- Do not make employee look careless.
- Deterministic clock graphic: `5:03 PM` or `After hours`.

Overlay:

`Your office is closed.`

Then:

`Your customers aren't always done calling.`

### 5-10 sec - Customer need appears

- Different location: homeowner / customer at home or roadside depending on generic service-business direction.
- Customer searches, then places a call.
- Do not rely on generated phone-screen text.

Overlay:

`New customer calling`

### 10-15 sec - Coverage gap

- Empty front office / phone rings with nobody present.
- The ring is audible and visually understandable.
- Do not imply the employee was supposed to be working after hours.

Overlay:

`The opportunity can wait...`

Then:

`...or keep looking.`

### 15-23 sec - Add coverage

Deterministic workflow:

`AFTER-HOURS AI AGENT`

`Answers`
→ `Captures need`
→ `Applies your rules`
→ `Books / routes next step`

Use booking vs routing based on the configured business workflow.

### 23-27 sec - Morning handoff

- Employee returns in morning.
- Organized opportunity appears in deterministic system card.
- Employee reviews it with context rather than digging through voicemails.

Overlay:

`Your team starts with context.`

### 27-30 sec - CTA

`Same team.`
`More coverage.`

`YourAiDepartment.ai`

## Audio direction

- Off-camera male narrator preferred unless another tested voice is materially better.
- First half: subtle evening/quiet-office bed and audible unattended ring.
- AI solution section: ring resolves; do not turn office into silence or sci-fi sound design.
- No spoken domain.

## Deterministic graphics

- After-hours clock
- New customer calling
- AI agent workflow
- Next-step card
- YAD CTA

## Primary QA gates

- Employee is not blamed for leaving at normal closing time.
- After-hours call is visually clear.
- Empty office ring is physically logical.
- AI is introduced only after coverage problem is understood.
- Booking/routing language is conditional on configured rules.
- No guarantees.
- Same-team / more-coverage message is clear.
