# Smartlead Outbound Experiment — Reply-First vs. Call-First

**Status:** designed, not yet run. Nothing in this document has been executed, and no result is claimed anywhere in this repository.

**Website work required to run it:** complete. `/go/law-firms/` and `/go/roofing/` are live in the build, tagged links are documented in `docs/analytics/smartlead-campaign-links.md`, and the measurement is in `docs/analytics/conversion-event-taxonomy.md`.

**Smartlead configuration required to run it:** not done. This repository has no Smartlead credentials or API integration, so the sequences below are a specification for a person to configure, not something that has been programmed.

---

## 1. What the August data actually says, and what it does not

Approximate figures from the two August sequences:

| | Law firms | Roofing |
|---|---|---|
| Leads | 361 | 292 |
| Sends | 786 | 602 |
| Opens | 299 (~51.8%) | 230 (~50.7%) |
| Reported clicks | 121 (~26.6%) | 227 (~50.0% of sends) |
| Replies | 0 | 0 |
| Positive replies | 0 | 0 |
| Lead bounces | 13 (~1.65%) | 2 (~0.33%) |
| Sender bounces | 66 (~8.4%) | 50 (~8.3%) |

**What can be said.** Deliverability is working — half of the recipients opened. The offer, the ask, or the landing experience is not producing a response.

**What cannot be said.** That 227 roofers clicked. A click count of 227 against 230 opens, with zero replies, is not a plausible human click rate on cold outbound. Security appliances, corporate link scanners, and privacy proxies all fetch URLs found in email, often at open time, and they are counted as clicks by every email platform. Roofing's ~50% and law firms' ~26.6% are also suspiciously far apart for two sequences of similar quality sent to similar list sizes, which is more consistent with a difference in recipients' mail infrastructure than a difference in copy.

**This is a hypothesis, not a finding.** It has not been tested, and it must not be written down anywhere as though it has. The measurement in §4 exists specifically to test it.

**A third possibility that matters.** Until 2026-08-31 the roofing sequence pointed at `https://youraidepartment.ai/assessment/`, a route that has never existed on this site. Those clicks reached a 404. That is now fixed with a 301, and it is a sufficient explanation for zero replies from that sequence on its own — which is another reason not to reach for the bot hypothesis first.

## 2. The change being tested

Both August sequences asked a cold prospect to complete a free 15-question assessment. That is a large ask of someone who did not initiate contact: the effort lands entirely on the person who was interrupted, before any human has said anything to them.

The experiment tests whether the ask is the problem, and if so, which smaller ask works better.

### Arm A — CONTROL: reply-first

**Email #1 contains no link at all.** The ask is a reply.

This is the control because it removes the click entirely. If the click is contaminated, this arm is unaffected by that contamination; a reply cannot be generated by a link scanner.

Law-firm concept:

> **Subject:** quick question about {{company}} intake
>
> {{first_name}}, when someone calls {{company}} after hours or your intake team is tied up, what happens to that lead?
>
> We build AI systems for law firms that can help answer, qualify, collect intake information, schedule consultations and update existing systems.
>
> I mapped out a few places a firm like yours may be able to automate.
>
> Want me to send them over?

Roofing concept:

> **Subject:** quick question about {{company}} call handling
>
> {{first_name}}, when a call comes in to {{company}} while your crews are on a roof — or at nine at night after a storm — what happens to it?
>
> We build AI systems for roofing companies that can help answer, capture the details, get the inspection on the calendar and keep follow-up moving in the CRM you already use.
>
> I looked at a few places a company your size may be able to automate.
>
> Want me to send them over?

The follow-up emails in this arm may contain a link to the `/go/` page, once the recipient has had a chance to reply. Only email #1 is strictly link-free.

Do not add a tracking pixel or read receipt to make this arm "measurable". Its measurement is the reply count, which is a far stronger signal than anything a pixel would produce.

### Arm B — CHALLENGER: call-first

**Email #1 links to the vertical's `/go/` page**, which asks for the 30-minute strategy call.

CTA concept: *See the 3 automations I'd look at first for {{company}}* → `/go/law-firms/` or `/go/roofing/`.

The link must carry the full tagged URL from `docs/analytics/smartlead-campaign-links.md` §4a.

### What is deliberately NOT being tested

- The assessment as the cold ask. Both August sequences already ran that, and the result is above. Re-running it as a third arm would spend list on a question already answered.
- Multiple offers at once. One variable at a time, or nothing is learned.

---

## 3. Splitting the list

Split **within each vertical**, not across them. Law firms and roofing have different list quality, different mail infrastructure, and different reply norms; comparing arm A on law firms against arm B on roofing would be comparing verticals, not arms.

- Randomise assignment, do not split by list order — imported lists are frequently ordered by source, region or company size.
- Same sending window, same sender accounts, same warm-up state for both arms. Sender reputation moves faster than any effect being measured here.
- Sender bounce is running ~8% on both sequences. That is a deliverability problem in its own right and worth fixing *before* the test, because a change in bounce rate mid-test will swamp the result.

## 4. What to measure — and what not to

| Metric | Where | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Replies | Smartlead | **Arm A's primary outcome.** Cannot be produced by a scanner |
| Positive replies | Smartlead | The number that actually matters in arm A |
| Reported clicks | Smartlead | Arm B only. **Do not use as the headline metric** |
| `cold_lp_view` | GA4 | A browser rendered the `/go/` page and ran JavaScript |
| `cold_lp_engaged` | GA4 | That browser stayed ≥15s visible, or interacted |
| `outbound_cta_click` (`cta_type=strategy_call`) | GA4 | Booking intent on the page |
| `outbound_cta_click` (`cta_type=assessment`) | GA4 | Chose to look before talking |
| `booking_confirmed` | GA4 | **Arm B's primary outcome.** A real booked call |
| Booked calls | Cal.com | The ground truth to reconcile `booking_confirmed` against |

### The click-quality diagnostic

For arm B, put these three numbers side by side, filtered to `utm_campaign` = the campaign id:

```
Smartlead reported clicks
GA4 cold_lp_view
GA4 cold_lp_engaged
```

Read it as follows, remembering that none of these readings is proof:

- **Clicks ≫ views** (e.g. 227 / 12). Consistent with most "clicks" being automated fetches that never rendered the page. Would mean the August click rates should never have been read as engagement, and that the sequence's real reach is far smaller than it looked.
- **Clicks ≈ views ≫ engaged** (e.g. 227 / 210 / 8). Real browsers arriving and leaving immediately. That is a landing-page or message-match problem, and it is fixable.
- **Clicks ≈ views ≈ engaged, still no bookings.** Real people are reading the page and not booking. That is an offer problem, and the page copy is the thing to change.

`cold_lp_view` requires JavaScript execution, which most link scanners do not do — but some do. **None of these readings is proof.** Each one narrows the question enough to decide what to change next, which is more than exists today.

### Numbers that must not be reported as sales

`booking_click_strategy` and `outbound_cta_click` are clicks toward a scheduler. GA4's Key Event count is whatever an operator ticked a box for. Neither is a booked call, and neither is a qualified lead. See `docs/analytics/conversion-event-taxonomy.md` §1.

## 5. Sample size, honestly

At the August volumes — roughly 600–800 sends per sequence — and with booked calls as the outcome, this test will **not** reach statistical significance on bookings. It is very unlikely to produce even a single-digit booking count per arm.

That is not a reason to skip it. It is a reason to be explicit about what it can and cannot settle:

**What this volume CAN answer:**

- Does a link-free email produce replies at all, when a linked one produced zero? Zero versus any is a real signal.
- Is the Smartlead click count reflecting browsers? The `cold_lp_view` comparison answers this at almost any volume, because the effect size — if it is real — is enormous.
- Do people who reach the `/go/` page stay on it? Engagement rate needs far fewer sessions than a booking rate does.

**What it CANNOT answer:**

- Which arm produces more booked calls per thousand sends. Not at this volume. Do not declare a winner on bookings.
- Whether one vertical is "better". Two list samples of a few hundred cannot support that.

**Decision rule.** Run for at least two full sequence cycles, or 30 days, whichever is longer. Then decide on the *diagnostic* — reply count and the click/view/engaged shape — not on bookings. Record the actual numbers, including zeros, in `docs/analytics/` when the run completes.

---

## 6. Compliance, since this is unsolicited mail

Not a legal review, and not a substitute for one. These are the operational floors:

- Every send carries a working, honest unsubscribe, and opt-outs are suppressed across **all** sequences, not just the one they replied to.
- A valid physical postal address in the footer.
- Subject lines and sender identity are accurate — no fake "Re:" threading, no impersonation.
- No prospect identifier in a URL. Not `{{email}}`, not `{{first_name}}`, not a per-recipient hash. UTM values reach GA4, referrer headers, and browser history; the individual belongs in Smartlead and the CRM.
- Sender bounce at ~8% is high enough to threaten domain reputation. Verify the list and fix that before scaling volume.

---

## 7. When the run completes

Write the result — including a null result — into `docs/analytics/`, with the actual counts and the date range. A test whose outcome is never recorded gets re-run by the next person from the same starting assumptions.
