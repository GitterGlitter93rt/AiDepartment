# 1. EXECUTIVE RESULT

The current YAD hook strategy is directionally strong.

The best release candidates are **specific business-process questions tied to truthful current context**. The weakest candidates are generic AI pitches, accusatory loss claims, product-first AI-receptionist questions, stacked discovery questions and over-researched/creepy openers.

Recommended release posture:

- **Primary tomorrow:** HVAC / Plumbing advertiser-first process hooks.
- **Secondary after voice validation:** Roofing unsold-estimate hook.
- **Do not lead with:** generic AI positioning, attribution unless stakeholder/context supports it, or broad admin-capacity questions.

The offline test supports the core YAD doctrine:

> business problem first, technology second.

---

# 2. METHOD

Each hook was reviewed against the 10-dimension offline score defined in:

`outbound-sales-brain-hook-backtest-framework.md`

Maximum: 50.

This score evaluates design quality only:

- truth safety;
- relevance;
- answerability;
- non-accusatory framing;
- spoken naturalness;
- discovery yield;
- role fit;
- low creepiness;
- brevity;
- fit with one shared YAD sales process.

It does **not** predict actual conversion rate.

Real-world evidence begins with controlled pilot calls.

# 4. WHY THE TOP HVAC HOOK WINS OFFLINE

Candidate:

> Quick cold call — I came across you guys while looking at companies advertising emergency AC around Jacksonville. When a new call hits after hours, what happens today?

Strengths:

1. immediately honest about cold context;
2. public current ad/service observation supplies a real reason for the call;
3. does not claim the advertising works or fails;
4. does not mention ad spend;
5. asks a question the owner/GM can answer instantly;
6. answer reveals the actual workflow;
7. natural next questions depend on what they say;
8. supports a strong no-sale outcome if their system is already excellent;
9. avoids leading with AI;
10. aligns with high-intent/emergency economics without inventing financial impact.

Expected first answers are operational:

- voicemail;
- answering service;
- dispatcher/on-call person;
- receptionist;
- direct booking;
- callback next morning;
- automated workflow.

Each gives the Sales AI something real to react to.

Best when:

## A2 — simultaneous overflow

> I came across you guys while looking at [category] companies advertising around [market]. When a new call comes in while everybody's already tied up, what happens next?

Best when:

- we want to investigate daytime overflow rather than nights.

## B1 — category-safe after-hours fallback

> I was looking at HVAC companies around [market]. Quick question — when somebody reaches out after hours, what happens today?

Use when current advertising evidence is not strong enough to state.

# 6. ROOFING / ESTIMATE HOOK

Candidate:

> Quick cold call — I had one question about your sales process. What normally happens to an estimate that doesn't close the first time?

Offline score: **48/50**.

Why it works:

- almost every estimate-driven company understands the question;
- no claim that they fail to follow up;
- reveals owner, cadence, CRM/process and visibility;
- naturally exposes whether follow-up depends on individual reps;
- lets a strong company explain its strong process without creating conflict.

Likely high-value answer:

> Sales guys handle their own estimates.

Natural next question:

> Got it. Do you have visibility into whether every one of those actually gets followed up consistently?

This is substantially stronger than:

> How many estimates are you losing every month?

because the latter presupposes loss.

# 7. COLLISION / ESTIMATE HOOK

Candidate:

> What normally happens to an estimate that doesn't turn into a repair right away?

Offline score: **45/50**.

Strong because it fits the shop's real customer journey without telling the shop it has a problem.

Potential branches:

- customer says waiting on insurance;
- shops do/don't follow unsold estimate;
- estimator owns it;
- CSR owns it;
- no centralized visibility;
- automated follow-up exists.

No production/safety repair judgments are needed.

---

# 8. REAL ESTATE / NURTURE HOOK

Candidate:

> When a homeowner isn't ready to list yet but might be in a few months, what keeps that relationship alive?

Offline score: **46/50**.

Strong because it is about the long-cycle sales problem, not AI.

Useful answers:

- agents remember manually;
- CRM drip;
- ISA team;
- no consistent process;
- database nurture;
- marketing automation.

It naturally reveals whether the process is centralized or agent-dependent.

# 9. LAW / AFTER-HOURS INTAKE

Candidate:

> What happens to a new inquiry that comes in after the office closes?

Offline score with correct marketing/practice context: **44/50**.

Strong operationally, but implementation must retain law boundaries:

- no legal advice;
- no case-merit statements;
- no automatic case acceptance promises;
- no conflict-clearance promises;
- confidentiality/governance acknowledged when relevant.

This is why one core Sales AI can still use vertical safety context without becoming a separate law-sales personality.

---

# 10. WHY ATTRIBUTION SCORES LOWER AS A FIRST HOOK

Candidate:

> Can you currently trace a lead from the original source all the way to actual revenue?

Offline score: **40/50**.

This is an excellent business question but:

- requires more thought;
- front-line personnel may not know;
- can sound like a marketing audit;
- less conversational in the first seconds.

Recommendation:

Use for:

- owner;
- GM;
- marketing leadership;
- a prospect already discussing marketing/CRM;
- backup hypothesis after initial engagement.

Do not make it the universal first hook.

---

# 11. WHY ADMIN CAPACITY SCORES LOWER AS A FIRST HOOK

Candidate:

> What repetitive office task eats more employee time than you think it should?

Offline score: **38/50**.

Good discovery question, but broad.

The prospect has to mentally search their operation before answering.

Better after context such as:

> You mentioned the office is still manually moving the intake data between systems. What part of that process eats the most time?

Recommendation:

Keep as discovery/backup; do not lead tomorrow's pilot with it.

# 12. NEGATIVE CONTROL FINDINGS

## Generic AI pitch — 26/50

> We're an AI automation company...

Fails because the prospect must first decide whether they care about AI rather than answering a business question.

## Product-first AI receptionist — 29/50

> Are you using an AI receptionist yet?

Prematurely prescribes one implementation and makes YAD look like a phone-bot seller.

## Stacked discovery — 22/50

Four questions at once is not a conversation.

## Accusatory loss hook — hard fail

> You're probably losing a lot of money from missed calls.

Unsupported accusation and poor trust posture.

## Creepy research dump — hard fail

Too much public data in the first seconds plus an unsupported missed-lead conclusion.

# 14. LIVE REVIEW AFTER EACH EARLY CALL

For the first calls, review:

1. Was the opener audible immediately?
2. Did it sound human/natural rather than generated?
3. Did the prospect understand the reason for the call?
4. Did they answer the first question?
5. Did the AI respond to their actual answer?
6. Did the AI ask one good follow-up rather than pitch?
7. Did it handle interruption correctly?
8. Did the conversation expose a real process fact?
9. Did it correctly decide meeting/no meeting?
10. Was the closing/booking flow natural?

If the opener itself works but the call fails later, do not rewrite the opener to fix a discovery/latency/booking problem.
