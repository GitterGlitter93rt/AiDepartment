# Your AI Department — 15-Minute Strategy Call Playbook

**Status:** Sales process authority  
**Date:** 2026-09-03  
**Host:** Michael Chanata  
**Purpose:** Convert a qualified cold-call handoff into a useful business decision without making the prospect repeat the entire first conversation or turning a 15-minute call into a generic AI presentation.

---

# 1. PURPOSE OF THE 15-MINUTE CALL

The call is not automatically a proposal meeting.

It exists to determine:

1. Is the problem real?
2. Is it meaningful enough to matter?
3. What does the current workflow actually look like?
4. What data/numbers are available?
5. What system/stakeholder constraints matter?
6. Is there a credible YAD next step?
7. If yes, what is the smallest sensible next step?

Valid outcomes:

- deeper discovery/assessment;
- Executive AI Strategy discussion when appropriate;
- defined implementation scoping path;
- service-specific conversation;
- follow-up after data collection;
- no sale.

---

# 2. PRE-CALL RULE

Michael should receive a prep brief before the meeting containing:

- company;
- attendee + role;
- why YAD called;
- original research context;
- exact workflow statements from prospect;
- problem/pain wording;
- systems/numbers prospect stated;
- objections/concerns;
- what the prospect expects from this call;
- questions still unanswered;
- things Michael must not assume.

Do not begin with:

> Tell me about your business.

The AI/human prospecting system should already know enough to start intelligently.

---

# 3. MINUTE 0–2 — OPEN / CONTINUITY

Recommended shape:

> John, good to meet you. I have the notes from the call you had with our team, so I won't make you start over. My understanding is [one-sentence factual recap]. Is that basically right?

Example:

> My understanding is your roofing reps each handle their own estimate follow-up and you don't have great visibility into whether every proposal gets worked consistently. Is that basically right?

Then correct immediately if prospect says no.

Agenda:

> Perfect. In 15 minutes I just want to understand how that works today, whether it's meaningful enough to fix, and what the simplest next step would be if there is one.

No slide deck required.

---

# 4. MINUTE 2–6 — MAP THE CURRENT WORKFLOW

Use workflow:

`trigger -> intake -> system -> owner -> action -> follow-up -> outcome -> measurement`.

Ask only what is missing from the prior call.

Examples:

- Where does the lead/estimate first get recorded?
- Who owns the next action?
- What tells them when to follow up?
- What happens if they don't do it?
- Can management see that today?
- What system is involved?

Do not ask questions the prospect already clearly answered on the cold call unless verifying an important fact.

---

# 5. MINUTE 6–10 — SIZE THE BUSINESS PROBLEM

Use prospect/company data when available.

Possible questions:

- Roughly how many of these occur in a normal month?
- What percentage are legitimate opportunities?
- What is a typical customer/job/case/proposal worth?
- What percentage realistically might be recoverable?
- How much employee time goes into the current process?
- What happens operationally when volume spikes?

Separate:

- theoretical exposure;
- realistic recoverable opportunity;
- implementation cost;
- expected measurement plan.

Do not turn uncertain numbers into guaranteed ROI.

If the prospect does not know:

> That's fine. The first step may simply be measuring it before we decide whether it's worth building anything.

---

# 6. MINUTE 10–13 — POSITION THE RIGHT CATEGORY

Only after current workflow/problem is understood.

Pattern:

> Based on what you're describing, I wouldn't start by replacing [existing system/person]. I'd look at [specific workflow category] around it.

Potential categories:

- CRM optimization;
- lead-routing/follow-up automation;
- AI phone/overflow support;
- estimate/proposal follow-up;
- reactivation;
- reporting/attribution;
- integration;
- employee/internal assistant;
- marketing/funnel improvement;
- process redesign;
- measurement-first.

Keep it conceptual if feasibility isn't proven.

Do not promise an integration before technical validation.

---

# 7. MINUTE 13–15 — CHOOSE THE NEXT STEP

## Outcome A — simple/clear opportunity

> This is specific enough that the next step is probably to scope the workflow and the systems it touches. I'll have the team put together exactly what we need from you to do that.

## Outcome B — broader/complex opportunity

Potential Executive AI Strategy path:

> There are enough moving pieces here that I wouldn't want to prescribe one automation from a 15-minute call. The better next step may be a deeper strategy engagement where we map the workflows, systems and economics properly.

Use current Commercial Truth for any pricing discussion.

## Outcome C — data missing

> Before I sell you anything, let's get the actual numbers. If we can measure [specific metric], we'll know whether this deserves investment.

## Outcome D — assessment useful

> The free AI Department Assessment would be useful here because there may be more than one workflow worth looking at. Let's use that to structure the next conversation.

## Outcome E — no sale

> Based on what you've shown me, I don't think this is something I'd force right now. Your current process sounds strong enough that the business case isn't obvious.

No-sale protects credibility.

---

# 8. DO NOT TURN 15 MINUTES INTO A GENERIC DEMO

Do not spend the call showing:

- generic ChatGPT tricks;
- every YAD service;
- a canned AI receptionist demo unrelated to their problem;
- technical architecture.

A demo is useful only when it answers the prospect's specific question and represents current capability truthfully.

---

# 9. IF THEY WANT TO SEE THE PHONE AI

If relevant:

> We can show you the phone experience, but I want to separate the demo from your actual implementation. Your workflow, systems, routing and rules would need to be scoped before I'd say what yours should do.

Then use Demo AI intentionally.

Do not present demo behavior as guaranteed production scope.

---

# 10. IF PROSPECT ASKS PRICE

Use current Commercial Truth.

Do not quote stale packages.

If scope unclear:

> I can give you the current range for the type of work, but I don't want to pretend I know your actual scope yet.

Separate:

- free Assessment;
- free 15-minute strategy conversation;
- Executive AI Strategy where appropriate;
- implementation/custom work;
- recurring services.

---

# 11. END WITH EXPLICIT OWNERSHIP

Every meeting ends with one of:

- Michael/YAD action + due date;
- prospect action + due date;
- next meeting booked;
- assessment sent;
- data request;
- no sale;
- revisit trigger/time.

Never end with vague:

> We'll circle back sometime.

---

# 12. MEETING NOTES

Persist:

- confirmed problem;
- workflow map;
- numbers/source;
- systems;
- stakeholders;
- current priority;
- constraints;
- next-step category;
- commitments;
- commercial discussion;
- technical questions requiring validation;
- no-sale reason when applicable.

Update the same canonical Account/Opportunity.

---

# 13. SUCCESS METRICS

Track:

- attendance rate;
- correct-stakeholder rate;
- meetings Michael marks properly qualified;
- problem confirmed rate;
- next-step rate;
- opportunity creation rate;
- no-sale rate;
- time-to-next-action;
- prospect-repeat-work score: how often Michael had to ask for information already captured.

A higher no-sale rate can be healthy if qualification quality improves.

---

# 14. CORE RULE

**The cold AI earns the 15 minutes. Michael uses the 15 minutes to determine whether a real business case exists — not to restart discovery or give a generic AI pitch.**
