## Core Position

Businesses should not begin by buying AI tools.

They should begin by identifying expensive, repetitive, slow, inconsistent, or difficult business problems and then determine whether AI, automation, software, process improvement, or some combination is appropriate.

AI is a means to improve a business system, not the objective itself.

## Reader Outcome

The reader should understand how to identify a sensible first AI initiative without chasing technology for its own sake.

## Proposed H1

Where Should a Business Start With AI?

## Suggested Introduction

Many companies start their AI journey by asking:

"What AI tools should we buy?"

That is usually the wrong first question.

The better question is:

"Where is the business losing time, money, capacity, consistency, or opportunities?"

Once the problem is understood, the company can determine whether AI belongs in the solution.

## Recommended Structure

### H2 — Start With the Business Problem, Not the AI Tool

Explain that AI initiatives should originate from measurable operational or commercial problems.

Potential examples:

- slow lead response
- repetitive administrative work
- inconsistent follow-up
- manual reporting
- information trapped across systems
- repetitive research
- difficult knowledge retrieval
- customer-service bottlenecks
- employee time spent on low-value tasks

Do not claim AI is appropriate for every example.

### H2 — Look for Work That Is Repetitive, Expensive, or Difficult to Scale

Explain characteristics that make a process worth evaluating.

Potential signals:

- performed frequently
- follows recognizable patterns
- consumes significant labor
- delays customers or employees
- produces inconsistent results
- requires information from multiple systems
- creates avoidable rework

### H2 — Understand the Current Process Before Automating It

Explain that automating a broken process can make the problem faster rather than better.

Map:

Current Process
→ Bottlenecks
→ Inputs
→ Decisions
→ Human Review
→ Output
→ Measurement

### H2 — Decide Whether AI Is Actually Necessary

Possible alternatives may include:

- better process design
- existing software features
- conventional automation
- CRM workflow improvements
- integrations
- better training
- clearer accountability

AI should be selected when it materially improves the solution.

### H2 — Start With a High-Value, Manageable Use Case

Good first projects should balance:

- business value
- implementation complexity
- risk
- data availability
- stakeholder support
- ability to measure results

Avoid starting with the most complex possible enterprise transformation.

### H2 — Define What Success Means Before Implementation

Potential measurements:

- time saved
- response time
- conversion rate
- throughput
- rework
- cost per completed task
- employee capacity
- customer wait time
- adoption

Do not invent benchmark percentages.

### H2 — Keep Humans in the Process Where Judgment Matters

Discuss:

- financial decisions
- legal implications
- sensitive communications
- high-value customer interactions
- safety issues
- ambiguous decisions
- final approvals

### H2 — A Practical First-Step Framework

Suggested framework:

1. Identify expensive friction.
2. Document the current process.
3. Estimate the value of improvement.
4. Determine whether AI is necessary.
5. Select a manageable use case.
6. Define human review.
7. Define measurement.
8. Pilot before scaling.
