## Audience

Business owners, executives, department leaders, and operations leaders evaluating where AI may create meaningful business value.

## Core Position

The best AI opportunities are not necessarily the most technically impressive.

They are business problems where improvement matters economically and where the process is suitable for AI, automation, or better systems.

## Reader Outcome

The reader should be able to create a shortlist of AI opportunities based on business impact, feasibility, risk, and measurability.

## Recommended Structure

### H2 — Begin With Friction

Look for:

- delays
- repetitive work
- manual handoffs
- missed follow-up
- information retrieval problems
- inconsistent processes
- high administrative burden
- unstructured information
- bottlenecks that limit growth

### H2 — Separate Interesting AI Ideas From Valuable Business Opportunities

An interesting demo is not necessarily a worthwhile implementation.

Evaluate:

- frequency
- economic impact
- labor intensity
- customer impact
- scalability
- risk
- implementation effort

### H2 — Score Opportunities Across Four Dimensions

Suggested framework:

#### Business Impact

How meaningful would improvement be?

#### Feasibility

Can the workflow, data, integrations, and tools support implementation?

#### Risk

What happens when the system is wrong?

#### Measurability

Can improvement actually be measured?

### H2 — Look Across the Entire Business

Potential departments:

- sales
- marketing
- customer service
- operations
- finance
- HR
- training
- knowledge management
- leadership
- administrative workflows

Avoid suggesting that every department needs AI.

### H2 — Prioritize Repeated Work

A small improvement to a process performed thousands of times can matter more than a dramatic improvement to a process performed twice per year.

Do not manufacture numerical examples unless clearly labeled as hypothetical.

### H2 — Evaluate Data and System Readiness

Questions:

- Where does the information live?
- Is the data structured?
- Is it reliable?
- Are systems integrated?
- Is information sensitive?
- Who owns the workflow?
- Can humans review outputs?

### H2 — Account for Change Management

A technically successful system can still fail if employees do not understand, trust, or use it.

Consider:

- workflow ownership
- training
- leadership support
- governance
- usability
- measurement

### H2 — Build an AI Opportunity Matrix

Suggested axes:

Business Impact
vs.
Implementation Complexity

Categories:

- Quick Wins
- Strategic Projects
- Low-Priority Opportunities
- Avoid / Reconsider
