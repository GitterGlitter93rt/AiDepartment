## Core Position

Automation should not be treated as automatically beneficial.

Some workflows require human judgment, accountability, empathy, context, legal review, or careful escalation.

The goal is not maximum automation.

The goal is the right division of work between people and technology.

## Recommended Structure

### H2 — Automation Is a Business Decision, Not a Technology Goal

Explain why maximum automation can create risk, poor customer experiences, and operational fragility.

### H2 — High-Stakes Decisions Need Human Accountability

Potential categories:

- legal decisions
- financial approvals
- hiring or termination decisions
- safety-critical decisions
- major contractual commitments
- sensitive customer disputes

Do not provide legal advice.

### H2 — Ambiguous Situations Often Need Human Judgment

AI performs best where the task and context are sufficiently defined.

Escalate unusual or uncertain situations.

### H2 — Sensitive Customer Conversations May Need People

Examples:

- complaints
- escalations
- emotionally difficult conversations
- large financial consequences
- complex negotiations

AI may assist preparation or routing without replacing the human interaction.

### H2 — Do Not Automate a Process You Do Not Understand

Before automation, document:

- inputs
- decisions
- exceptions
- ownership
- desired output
- failure modes

### H2 — Poor Data Can Make Automation Dangerous

Discuss inaccurate, incomplete, outdated, or inconsistent data.

### H2 — Some Tasks Should Be AI-Assisted, Not AI-Controlled

Examples:

AI drafts → Human reviews → Human approves

AI summarizes → Human interprets

AI recommends → Human decides

AI flags → Human investigates

### H2 — Use Escalation Thresholds

Define cases where the system should stop and request human intervention.

Potential triggers:

- uncertainty
- policy exception
- large financial impact
- sensitive information
- unusual customer request
- low-confidence output

### H2 — A Better Question Than "Can We Automate This?"

Use:

"Which parts of this process should technology handle, and which parts require human judgment?"
