Energy organizations often hold critical operational knowledge across manuals, policies, procedures, training materials, project files, and — perhaps most valuably — in the heads of experienced employees who've never written any of it down. AI-assisted knowledge systems can meaningfully improve access to approved information, as long as they respect existing permissions and never override the authority of the actual source documents.

## Knowledge Challenges

Information scattered across different repositories, undocumented knowledge that exists only in experienced employees' heads, slow internal search, inconsistent onboarding, difficulty finding the currently-correct version of a procedure, and different teams relying on different sources of truth are all common, real problems worth solving.

## Retrieval Architecture

A working system starts from approved sources, respects existing permissions, retrieves relevant material, generates an AI response grounded in that material, shows source citations or visibility so employees can verify it, and leaves room for employee review before anything gets treated as final.

## Appropriate Use

AI can help employees find the right procedure, locate a specific document, summarize approved material, compare internal references, support onboarding, assist with training, and draft internal documentation — all genuinely useful, low-risk applications.

## Guardrails

Access controls should always be respected. The system should prefer current, approved source material over anything else. Source context should be visible wherever possible. General model knowledge should never be allowed to override an authoritative internal procedure. And genuine uncertainty should route to a person rather than get papered over with a confident-sounding guess.
