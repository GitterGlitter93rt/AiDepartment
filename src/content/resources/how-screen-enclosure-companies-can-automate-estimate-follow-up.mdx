Sending a screen enclosure estimate feels like progress, but it doesn't create revenue by itself. Between the estimate and a signed contract, a homeowner might be comparing quotes, dealing with HOA approval requirements, working through permitting questions, considering financing, deciding on materials, or just taking longer than expected to make the call. Every one of those delays is a place an unstructured follow-up process quietly loses the deal.

## Why Estimates Go Quiet

Customers comparison shop, timing shifts, project scope changes after a second look, budgets get reconsidered, permitting questions come up, financing takes longer than expected, and — often — the salesperson themselves simply doesn't follow up consistently once they're juggling several other active proposals. None of these mean the deal is dead; most of them just mean it needs another nudge at the right time.

## Build a Follow-Up Workflow

A structured sequence handles this without depending on memory: the estimate goes out, gets acknowledged, creates a task for the salesperson, triggers a scheduled follow-up, delivers useful information if a question came up, leads to a real human conversation, and — if the customer isn't ready — moves into longer-term nurture instead of disappearing from the pipeline.

## Where AI Helps

AI can draft contextual follow-up messages based on where a specific deal stands, summarize previous communication so nothing gets repeated, flag unanswered questions, create tasks automatically, categorize which opportunities have stalled, and keep long-term nurture running in the background.

## Where Humans Matter

Pricing, design decisions, structural questions, permitting specifics, engineering, site conditions, and negotiation all require your team's real expertise — automation supports the process around those conversations, it doesn't replace the person having them.
