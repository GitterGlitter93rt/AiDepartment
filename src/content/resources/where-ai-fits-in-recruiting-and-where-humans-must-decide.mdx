The strongest recruiting use cases for AI usually involve repetitive coordination and information management, not replacing the actual hiring judgment. AI can help gather, organize, summarize, communicate, and schedule — the parts of recruiting that consume a recruiter's day without requiring their actual expertise. People should remain responsible for the decisions that genuinely affect someone's employment.

## The Recruiting Workflow

A typical path runs from application to intake, to initial screening, to candidate communication, to recruiter review, to an interview, to a hiring decision, and into onboarding. AI has a reasonable role in several of these stages — but not all of them.

## Good Candidates for AI Assistance

Acknowledging applicants, asking approved job-related screening questions, handling candidate communication, scheduling interviews, sending reminders, producing transcripts, preparing candidate summaries, creating recruiter tasks, generating onboarding checklists, and supporting internal recruiting reports are all reasonable, low-risk places for AI to genuinely help.

## Where Humans Should Remain Responsible

Hiring decisions, rejection decisions, compensation decisions, sensitive employment situations, accommodations, unusual candidate circumstances, and interpreting subjective or high-context information about a candidate should stay with qualified people — every time, without exception.

## Structured Screening

Screening questions should focus specifically on legitimate, job-related information. It's worth being deliberate about this: don't casually collect sensitive or protected information just because a screening conversation makes it easy to ask.

## Human Review

AI-generated candidate summaries can genuinely help a recruiter move faster, but they should never be treated as an infallible assessment of a candidate — they're a starting point for human review, not a substitute for it.

## Implementation

Where an ATS already exists, AI assistance and human review layer on top of the existing recruiting workflow. Where no ATS exists yet, the right next step might be implementing one, building a lighter structured recruiting database, or starting with scheduling and communication automation and adding more structure as hiring volume grows.
