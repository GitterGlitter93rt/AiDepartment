Energy organizations should start their AI adoption with controlled business workflows — improving information access, employee productivity, document processing, reporting, and coordination — without ever taking authority over safety-critical or regulated operations. The starting point should feel almost boring: better access to the right information, not a dramatic operational transformation.

## Candidate Areas

Internal knowledge, document processing, administrative operations, field-support information, project coordination, vendor workflows, reporting, employee training, and customer communication are all reasonable places to look for a genuinely useful first use case.

## Prioritization

Assess business value, how frequently the process actually occurs, the current manual workload, whether the underlying data is available, integration complexity, the safety consequence of an error, the regulatory consequence, and how much human oversight the use case realistically needs.

## Data and System Boundaries

Before implementation, be explicit about what data is permitted, where the AI system is allowed to run, which systems it can actually access, whether it needs write access to anything, and who's responsible for reviewing its output.

## Start Narrow

A controlled use case, working with approved information, assisting a defined set of employees, reviewed by a person, measured against a real objective, and only then expanded — this pattern reduces risk while still delivering real value quickly.

## Human Judgment

Safety-critical operations, engineering decisions, system controls, emergency response, regulated actions, and environmental decisions all require qualified human oversight — no AI implementation should ever be positioned as changing that.
