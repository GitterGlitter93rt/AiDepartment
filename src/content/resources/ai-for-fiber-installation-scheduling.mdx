Signing a customer up for fiber service isn't the finish line — it's the start of a physical installation process that has to actually happen before that customer becomes activated, paying revenue. Scheduling, reminders, rescheduling, and the installation handoff directly shape whether that new customer's first real experience with your company is smooth or frustrating.

## Sales-to-Install Handoff

Once a customer agrees to service, the handoff needs to confirm actual service availability at the address, get an installation appointment booked, and send a clear confirmation — closing the gap between "customer said yes" and "installation is actually scheduled" as quickly as possible.

## Reminders

From there, appointment confirmations, an advance reminder as the date approaches, clear arrival-window expectations, and any preparation requirements the customer needs to know about all reduce the chance of a missed or frustrating appointment.

## No-Show and Reschedule Recovery

When an appointment is missed anyway — and some always will be — an automated workflow should identify the miss, contact the customer, offer an approved rescheduling path, notify the internal install team, and update the system so nobody is left assuming an installation happened when it didn't. Treating a no-show as its own defined workflow, rather than an exception someone has to notice and manually chase, is what keeps these from quietly falling through the cracks.

## Human Escalation

Complex property issues, unusual installation conditions, or anything outside the routine scheduling flow should go straight to a person who can actually assess the situation — automation handles the routine scheduling logistics, not the judgment calls a technician or installer needs to make on-site.
