Cost per lead is one of the least useful numbers a solar company can optimize for in isolation. A campaign with a low cost per lead but a poor close rate can be far more expensive, in real terms, than one with a higher cost per lead that reliably produces closed customers. The more meaningful path to track runs from campaign to qualified lead, to appointment, through the sales process, to a closed customer and the revenue that customer represents.

## Building the Full Picture

This requires several pieces working together: accurate lead source data, UTM parameters applied consistently across campaigns, call tracking for phone-driven inquiries, a CRM that preserves that source information as the lead moves through your pipeline, appointment outcomes recorded reliably, clear rep ownership of each opportunity, and closed-customer reporting that ties back to where the customer actually came from.

## Offline Conversion Feedback

Where your ad platforms support it, feeding closed-customer outcomes back into Google Ads or Meta as offline conversions lets the platform's own optimization understand which campaigns produce real customers — not just clicks or form fills. This is one of the more underused levers in solar marketing, largely because it requires the CRM and CRM discipline to actually produce clean, timely outcome data.

## Timing Reality

It's worth being upfront about a timing quirk specific to this industry: the initial sales event, the signed contract, the installation, and recognized revenue can happen at meaningfully different points in time depending on your business process — sometimes weeks or months apart. Attribution reporting should reflect that reality rather than assume revenue lands the same day a deal closes.
