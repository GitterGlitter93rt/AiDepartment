Most brokerages and teams are sitting on years of accumulated contacts — past buyer inquiries, seller prospects, open-house attendees, old website leads, former clients, and long-term nurture contacts that never quite converted. That database has real value, but only if it's segmented and followed up with intelligently, rather than treated as a list to blast whenever someone remembers it exists.

## Segment Before You Reach Out

A past client is a different conversation than a five-year-old website lead who never responded. A seller prospect is a different conversation than someone who attended one open house and went quiet. Segmentation by contact type and by how long it's been since meaningful engagement is the first step — treating the whole database as one audience is how re-engagement campaigns end up feeling generic and get ignored.

## The Workflow

From the database, contacts move through segmentation, an eligibility and permission check, personalized outreach appropriate to who they actually are, a response, a handoff to a human agent, and ideally an appointment. Skipping the permission check is a real risk here — not every old record should automatically receive fresh outreach.

## AI's Role

AI can summarize historical notes so an agent doesn't have to dig through years of CRM entries, categorize contacts by type and recency, draft outreach appropriate to the segment, identify a likely next action, create tasks for agents, and maintain the nurture cadence over time.

## Guardrails

Consent, opt-out preferences, brokerage policies, fair-housing considerations, and professional judgment all apply just as much to a five-year-old contact as to a brand-new lead. Reactivation should feel like a relevant, well-timed outreach to someone with real prior interest — not a mass campaign to everyone who ever gave you an email address.
