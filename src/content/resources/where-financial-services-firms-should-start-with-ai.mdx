A financial-services organization shouldn't start its AI adoption by choosing a model or buying the newest AI product on the market. The better starting point is identifying a specific business workflow where AI can create genuine value, while respecting data sensitivity, access controls, regulatory obligations, and human accountability from the very first decision.

## Start With the Business Process

Internal knowledge, document review, customer-service assistance, operational reporting, employee productivity, workflow routing, marketing operations, and administrative processing are all reasonable candidate areas — the right one depends entirely on where your organization's actual friction lives.

## Evaluate Data Sensitivity

Before implementing anything, it's worth being explicit about what data is actually involved: is customer information present? Is confidential business information present? Who should have access? Is the specific model or vendor actually approved for that classification of data? What information should never leave controlled systems under any circumstances?

## Evaluate Decision Risk

Low-consequence assistance — drafting, summarizing, retrieving information — is a very different risk category than anything touching a high-consequence financial or regulated decision. Being clear about which category a use case falls into should happen before implementation, not after.

## Prioritization Framework

Weigh business value, workflow volume, how repetitive the work genuinely is, whether the data is actually ready to use, integration complexity, the consequence of a wrong decision, security requirements, and how much human review the use case realistically needs.

## Start With a Controlled Use Case

A defined use case, using approved data, for a defined set of users, producing a clear output, reviewed by a person, measured against a real objective, and rolled out under active monitoring is a far safer starting pattern than a broad, loosely-scoped experiment.

## Build vs. Buy

Existing enterprise software, approved AI platforms, secure knowledge tools, and workflow automation should all be evaluated before assuming custom development is necessary — custom integration is justified when the existing landscape genuinely can't do the job, not by default.

## Human Judgment

AI should never be presented as independently making regulated financial, credit, fiduciary, compliance, legal, or investment decisions — that responsibility remains with licensed professionals throughout.
