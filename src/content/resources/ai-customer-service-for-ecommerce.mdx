Customer service is usually the first place e-commerce brands consider AI, and for good reason — a large share of support volume is genuinely repetitive. The question that actually matters isn't whether to automate support, it's which specific interactions are good candidates and which ones need to stay with a person.

## Good Candidates for Automation

Order status, shipment tracking, common product questions, approved policy information, basic return-process guidance, and routine account questions are all high-volume, low-ambiguity interactions where a fast, accurate automated answer genuinely serves the customer better than waiting in a queue.

## Human Escalation

Damaged or lost orders, an angry customer, an unusual refund request, anything with a fraud concern, a complex product problem, a high-value customer relationship, or a request that falls outside your stated policy should all route to a person. These are exactly the situations where a generic automated response tends to make things worse, not better.

## Data Connection

For automated responses to actually be trustworthy, the system needs to retrieve verified order information, real product data, actual shipping status, your real policies, and customer history where appropriate — pulled from your real systems, not generated from a general sense of what an e-commerce policy "usually" says.

## Never Invent

AI should never fabricate shipment status, approve a refund it has no authority to approve, guess at inventory availability, invent a delivery date, overstate product capabilities, or state a policy that isn't actually your policy. Every one of those is a trust failure that a customer will remember far longer than the convenience of a fast automated reply.

The best e-commerce support systems aren't the ones that automate the most — they're the ones that automate the right things accurately and hand off everything else to a person quickly.
