Real estate organizations frequently know exactly where a lead originated — a portal, a Google Ads campaign, an open house — and then lose that attribution somewhere in the months between the initial inquiry and an eventual closing. Long sales cycles and multiple agent handoffs make this a genuinely harder attribution problem than most industries face, but it's still solvable with the right process.

## Capture Source

The starting point is consistent capture across every channel — portal, Google Ads, Meta, organic search, referral, open house, direct website inquiry, or agent-generated — recorded the same way regardless of source, so it can actually be compared later.

## Preserve Source Through the CRM

The harder discipline is making sure that source information doesn't get overwritten or lost when a lead gets assigned to an agent or team. This is the single most common place real estate attribution breaks down — the data existed at intake and then quietly disappeared once a human took over the relationship.

## Track Meaningful Milestones

From there, the milestones worth tracking are a qualified lead, an appointment, a showing, a listing meeting, an active opportunity, a signed contract, and the eventual closing. Each milestone is a checkpoint where you can see whether a particular source is producing real progress or just early-stage interest that never goes anywhere.

## Use Reporting for Better Decisions

With that data in place, you can actually answer which sources produce appointments, which produce closings (a meaningfully different question), how individual agents or teams perform with comparable lead sources, what real cost of acquisition looks like once conversion is accounted for, and how much a long-cycle lead is ultimately worth once it finally closes. None of this supports invented ROI figures — the value is in the visibility itself, not a fabricated return-on-investment number.
