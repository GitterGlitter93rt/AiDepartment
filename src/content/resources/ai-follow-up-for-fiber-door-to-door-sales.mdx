A field rep knocking on doors in a new fiber territory generates real interest — but that interest only has value if it becomes structured customer data immediately, not a name scrawled on a clipboard that may or may not make it back to the office.

## Capture

The capture step should record the address, the resident or contact, which representative made contact, the territory, whether the address actually has service availability, the level of interest, and the customer's preferred next action. This is the foundation everything else depends on — territory launches move fast, and inconsistent capture at the door creates gaps that are hard to fix later.

## Follow-Up

From there, a structured sequence — confirmation, SMS or email, a task for the rep, a phone call, scheduling, and a nurture track for anyone not ready yet — keeps the relationship going without relying entirely on the rep remembering to circle back.

## Territory Visibility

Management needs to see contacts, leads, appointments, installations, and activations broken out by rep and by territory. This is what actually lets a fast-moving territory launch be managed with real data instead of a general sense of how canvassing is going.

## Responsible Communication

None of this should turn into uncontrolled outreach. Consent, opt-out requirements, and applicable telemarketing rules should be respected in every automated follow-up — this is a system for following up with real, already-contacted prospects, not a mechanism for mass unsolicited communication.
