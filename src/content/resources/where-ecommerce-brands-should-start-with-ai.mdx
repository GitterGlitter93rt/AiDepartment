E-commerce brands face a genuinely broad set of possible AI use cases — customer service, lifecycle marketing, merchandising, creative production, reporting, product data, internal knowledge, and operational administration all offer plausible entry points. That breadth is actually the problem: the best starting point usually isn't whichever use case seems newest or most exciting, it's the specific workflow where improving speed, quality, customer experience, or decision-making would create meaningful, measurable value for your specific business.

## Candidate Areas

Each of the areas above has real merit for some brands and little relevance for others. A brand with heavy support ticket volume gets far more from customer-service automation than one with minimal support load; a brand with a large, underused email list gets more from lifecycle marketing than one running a lean, already-optimized flow.

## A Prioritization Framework

Evaluate candidate use cases against business value, the actual volume of the underlying task, how repetitive that work genuinely is, whether the necessary data is available and reliable, how difficult the integration would be, the risk to the customer if something goes wrong, and how much human review the use case realistically requires.

## Start Narrow

The strongest starting pattern is one workflow, with a clearly defined and measurable objective, implemented in a controlled way, measured honestly, and only then expanded once it's proven out. Trying to improve five things at once usually means none of them get the attention needed to actually work well.

## Build vs. Buy

Before building anything custom, it's worth checking whether your existing platform — Shopify, Klaviyo, Gorgias, or whatever else you already run — already supports a lighter-weight version of what you're trying to do. Custom automation earns its cost when the existing tools genuinely can't do the job, not by default.
