A home service company can spend heavily on advertising to generate phone calls, and still lose a meaningful share of that spend the moment staff can't answer every one of them. HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, pest control, garage doors, pools, restoration, landscaping, and most other established home-service trades share this exact problem — high call volume paired with limited answering capacity.

## Why Calls Get Missed

After-hours demand, sudden call spikes, dispatchers already on other calls, field teams unavailable, storm-driven surges, seasonal demand, and simple shift changes or lunch breaks all create windows where a real call goes unanswered — and in most of these trades, an unanswered call usually just means the caller tries the next company.

## Immediate Recovery Workflow

When a call is missed, an automated acknowledgment, basic information collection, an offer to book an approved appointment or arrange a callback, an internal notification, and prompt human follow-up recover a real share of what would otherwise become a lost job.

## AI-Assisted Answering

Collecting customer and contact information, identifying a general service category, capturing the property location, noting a preferred appointment window, providing routine approved information, and routing the call appropriately are all reasonable tasks for AI-assisted answering to handle reliably.

## Human Escalation

Technical diagnosis, safety judgment, genuine emergencies, pricing exceptions, unusual customer situations, regulated work, and complaints should all reach a qualified person — automation's job is making sure that happens quickly, not handling these situations itself.

## Measurement

Tracking a missed call through to a recovered conversation, a booking, a completed job, and revenue where the data supports it gives you a real sense of whether your recovery system is actually working — without resorting to unsupported claims about exactly what percentage of calls get "recovered."
