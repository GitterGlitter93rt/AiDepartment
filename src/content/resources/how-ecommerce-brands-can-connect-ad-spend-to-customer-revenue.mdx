The first purchase a customer makes isn't the full picture of what an acquisition channel is actually worth. A campaign that produces customers who buy once and never return is a very different result than one that produces customers who become repeat buyers over months or years — and evaluating acquisition only by the first conversion misses that difference entirely.

## Acquisition Sources

Google Ads, Meta Ads, organic search, email, affiliates, and other channels all contribute to acquisition, often overlapping across the same customer's actual path to purchase.

## The Measurement Path Worth Building

A more complete view connects campaign to session or customer, to the order, to any repeat purchase, and to the customer's cumulative value over time — not just the value of the first transaction.

## Attribution Reality

It's worth being honest about the limitations here: customers touch multiple channels before purchasing, they switch devices, privacy changes have genuinely reduced tracking precision across the industry, different ad platforms report attribution differently from each other, and repeat purchases can happen well after the platform's own attribution window has closed. Any attribution system that presents its numbers as perfectly deterministic is overselling its own precision.

## Operational Data

Getting a reasonably reliable picture requires your storefront platform, analytics, the advertising platforms themselves, your email or SMS platform, your customer database, and your order data all contributing consistent, connected information.

## Management Questions Worth Answering

With that data in place, you can start asking which channels create genuinely new customers, which create customers who come back and buy again, which specific products tend to acquire the most valuable long-term customers, and — importantly — which campaigns look strong only inside the ad platform's own dashboard but don't hold up once repeat purchase behavior is factored in.
