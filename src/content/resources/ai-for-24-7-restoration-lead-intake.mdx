Water damage doesn't wait for 9 a.m. Neither does a fire, a burst pipe during a holiday weekend, or a storm that knocks out power at 2 a.m. and floods a basement by sunrise. Restoration is one of the few industries where a meaningful share of the highest-value, most urgent inquiries genuinely happen outside normal business hours — and a business without a real after-hours intake process is simply unavailable for exactly the calls that matter most.

## Why Emergency Intake Is Different

A restoration inquiry carries urgency, an often-distressed customer, a specific location, a particular type of loss, active ongoing damage in many cases, questions about service availability, sometimes real safety concerns, and a need for a clean handoff to whoever responds next. Getting all of that right in a single after-hours call is a lot to ask of an answering service reading from a generic script — but it's exactly what a well-designed AI-assisted intake workflow can help with.

## What AI Can Capture

A structured intake conversation can reliably capture caller identity, the property address, callback information, a general description of the type of loss, roughly when it happened, and enough basic routing information to get the right team engaged. Where appropriate, it can also capture insurance or carrier information. What it should not do is interpret coverage, offer adjusting advice, or make any judgment about what an insurance policy will or won't pay for — that's outside AI's role and, frankly, outside anyone's role except a licensed adjuster.

## Routing

Once basic information is captured, it needs to go to the right place — which in restoration can mean routing by market, service type, geography, a specific after-hours team, an available estimator or technician, or a genuine emergency-escalation path for situations that can't wait for a callback.

## Human Escalation

Some situations need a person immediately, not after intake finishes. Safety concerns, a major loss, unusual circumstances, coverage questions, complex property conditions, or a customer who clearly needs more support than a structured intake flow can provide should all trigger immediate human involvement — the system's job is to get that escalation to happen fast, not to handle it itself.

The goal of 24/7 intake isn't to remove people from the emergency response process — it's to make sure the phone actually gets answered, correctly, at 2 a.m., so the right person can take over from there.
