A single pest control lead can be worth very different amounts depending on what happens after the first visit. A customer who books once and never returns is a very different outcome from one who becomes a recurring quarterly account for years. Measuring marketing performance by raw lead count treats those two outcomes as identical, which they clearly aren't.

## The Full Chain

The more useful measurement runs from campaign to inquiry, to booking, to first service, to recurring-customer status, and finally to the revenue that recurring relationship produces over time — not just the value of the first appointment.

## Building the Data

This requires Google Ads and organic search data tied to inquiries, consistent tracking on calls and forms, a CRM or customer-management system that records recurring account status accurately, and — where your ad platforms support it — offline conversion tracking that feeds real outcomes back into campaign optimization.

## Management Questions Worth Answering

With that in place, you can answer which sources actually create booked customers, which create customers who become recurring accounts (a meaningfully higher bar), which campaigns are generating a lot of calls that don't convert well, and which markets show stronger retention once a customer signs up.
