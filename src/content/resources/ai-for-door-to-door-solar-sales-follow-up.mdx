Door-to-door canvassing is expensive — it requires paying and managing a field team, covering territory, and having enough real conversations to find genuinely interested homeowners. All of that value evaporates if an interested homeowner's information ends up in a rep's personal notes app instead of a system anyone else can act on.

## Capture the Interaction

A structured capture step records the homeowner and contact information, the address, which representative made contact, the territory, a general sense of interest level, the requested next step, and the homeowner's permissioned communication preference. This is the raw material every following step depends on — if it's inconsistent or missing, nothing downstream can work well.

## Trigger the Next Step

Once a lead is captured, the same kind of structured sequence used for any other channel applies: confirmation, a notification to the rep or team responsible, appointment scheduling, reminders, follow-up, and a recorded outcome. The difference from a paid-media lead is that this homeowner already had a real conversation with a person — the follow-up should build on that relationship, not restart cold.

## Rep Accountability

Visibility into leads created, appointments set, follow-up completed, outcomes, and performance by territory gives management a clear picture of which reps and which areas are actually converting field activity into real pipeline — information that's nearly impossible to assemble reliably from paper notes and personal phones.

## AI Assistance

AI can summarize field notes into something usable, generate contextual follow-up based on what the rep actually discussed, assist with scheduling, and help prioritize leads that have gone quiet longer than they should have. It should not be used to automate regulated or high-stakes sales representations — the actual sales conversation and any specific claims made to a homeowner remain the rep's responsibility.
