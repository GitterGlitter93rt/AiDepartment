Transportation and logistics operations run on documents — bills of lading, proof-of-delivery paperwork, invoices, customer instructions, carrier documents, internal forms, and general shipment paperwork all need to be reviewed, classified, entered into a system, compared against other records, routed to the right place, or attached to the correct operational record. None of these document types are interchangeable, and none should be treated as automatically suitable for unsupervised automated interpretation.

## AI Assistance

Document classification, field extraction, consistent naming, routing, matching against existing records, summarization, and flagging missing information are all reasonable tasks for AI to take on — reducing the manual re-keying and sorting that eats real time in a back office.

## Validation

Important extracted fields should be validated against the original source document, existing system data, defined business rules, and human review wherever the stakes justify it. Extraction without validation just moves errors faster, it doesn't reduce them.

## Workflow

A document arrives, gets classified, has relevant fields extracted, goes through validation, updates the appropriate system, and either completes cleanly or lands in an exception queue for a person to resolve.

## Implementation Risks

Poor-quality scans, inconsistent document formats across different carriers or customers, genuinely ambiguous fields, duplicate documents, and incorrect matching against the wrong system record are all real risks worth planning for explicitly — along with making sure any sensitive information in these documents is handled appropriately throughout.
