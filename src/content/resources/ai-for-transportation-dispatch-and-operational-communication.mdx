Dispatch teams spend a substantial share of their day just moving information around — between customers, operations staff, drivers, and whatever business systems are supposed to reflect what's actually happening. AI can genuinely help with that communication and coordination burden, without taking over the safety-critical judgment that dispatch actually exists to provide.

## Typical Information Flow

A request moves from customer to operations, to a dispatcher, to a driver, generates a status update, and flows back to the customer — a chain with a lot of manual re-typing and re-explaining at each handoff, most of which is genuinely repetitive.

## AI-Assisted Tasks

Summarizing conversations, extracting requested actions, creating tasks, drafting routine status updates, organizing shipment status, preparing handoff notes between shifts, retrieving approved customer requirements, and classifying exceptions are all reasonable places for AI to reduce the manual load.

## Exception Management

AI can surface a delayed shipment, missing information, a customer escalation, an appointment issue, a document problem, or an unresolved communication thread — flagging these for a person rather than pretending to understand every operational consequence automatically.

## Human Role

Dispatchers and operations teams remain responsible for safety decisions, any route or driver decision with real operational consequences, actually resolving exceptions, regulatory concerns, and unusual customer situations that need real judgment.
