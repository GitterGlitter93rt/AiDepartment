Maintenance requests arrive through phone calls, email, a resident portal, SMS, forms, and after-hours channels — often with incomplete information that creates unnecessary back-and-forth before anyone can actually act on the request. AI-assisted intake works best when it focuses on organizing that information and coordinating the workflow, not attempting to diagnose what's actually wrong with the property.

## Information AI Can Help Capture

A structured intake step can collect resident and contact information, the property and unit, a general issue category, a description of the problem, when it started, photos where your system supports them, access information for a technician or vendor, and the resident's preferred contact method.

## Triage and Routing

From there, a request can be categorized as routine maintenance, something requiring an outside vendor, an internal maintenance matter, something needing property-manager review, or a possible emergency requiring escalation. Any safety-critical determination should trigger human escalation rather than being decided by the system itself — AI should never be positioned as making that call independently.

## Vendor and Team Handoff

The request moves through structured intake, gets routed appropriately, gets assigned to the right person or vendor, gets scheduled, triggers a resident update, and eventually reaches a resolved status — a clear chain instead of a request that disappears into an inbox until someone happens to notice it.

## Human Escalation

Emergencies, safety issues, disputes, unclear conditions, sensitive resident situations, unusual property damage, and anything with financial or legal implications should all go to a person — the intake system's job is making sure that handoff happens quickly, not handling the situation itself.
