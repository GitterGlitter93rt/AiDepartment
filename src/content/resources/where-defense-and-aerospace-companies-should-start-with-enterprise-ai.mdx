Defense and aerospace organizations should begin AI adoption with approved business and knowledge workflows — places where security boundaries, data permissions, human accountability, and organizational value can all be clearly defined. The highest-consequence technical systems are exactly the wrong place to start.

## Appropriate Starting Areas

Internal knowledge, employee training, document administration, supplier and project administration, approved reporting, internal research assistance, repetitive business workflows, and general enterprise productivity are all reasonable, lower-risk places to begin.

## Data Classification

Before any implementation, an organization needs to determine whether the information involved is public, internal, proprietary, export-controlled, classified, contract-restricted, or otherwise sensitive. This is not a determination we make for you — your organization's own compliance and security functions must own that classification and the controls that follow from it.

## System Boundary

A clear implementation defines the approved model, the approved hosting or deployment environment, the approved data, the specific authorized users, their permissions, and how outputs are handled once produced.

## Human Review

Consequential actions require a human review step before they happen — this isn't optional in this environment.

## Start With a Controlled Pilot

A specific business workflow, using approved data, for defined users, running on governed AI infrastructure, with human review, real measurement, and only then controlled expansion — this is the pattern that actually works in a security-conscious organization.
