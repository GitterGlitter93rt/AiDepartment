Hardscapes, patios, pavers, outdoor kitchens, full landscape renovations, and design-build projects all share a common trait: long consideration cycles and, often, more than one competing bid. Follow-up on these projects needs to stay consistent through weeks or months of decision-making — without turning what should feel like a considered, personal process into something that feels like a generic sales funnel.

## The Workflow

The estimate goes out, gets acknowledged, triggers scheduled follow-up, delivers useful project information along the way, leads to a real human conversation, and results in either a decision or a longer-term nurture track if the homeowner needs more time.

## Getting the Tone Right

For a project this size, follow-up should feel like genuine interest in helping the homeowner move forward — not a countdown timer pushing them to decide. The cadence and message content matter as much as the fact that follow-up happens at all; a generic "still interested?" text undercuts the same premium positioning the project itself is selling.
